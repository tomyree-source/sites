Saiku Ruins Reveal a Seiden Built to the Capital's Standards — Palace of the Saio Who Served Ise Jingu

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: the announcement of the Saiku History Museum (November 4, 2026) and the Ise Shimbun (November 5, 2026). At the nationally designated historic site of the Saiku Ruins in Takegawa, Meiwa Town, Mie Prefecture, part of a building believed to have been the palace of the Saio of the Nara period has been newly discovered. The excavation is part of the museum's research to clarify the true nature of the Saiku site, and this is its 213th round.

【The June–September Survey — A Building South of the Precinct Confirmed】In the survey carried out from mid-June to September this year, researchers confirmed the remains of one building inside the Nishi-sei-hoi ward — a precinct thought to date from the Nara period — where nearly square postholes about 1.7 to 2 meters on a side stood in a row at intervals of about 3.2 meters (11 shaku). These are said to be the largest postholes confirmed so far at the site.

【The Survey Since October — A Seiden at the Center of the Precinct?】When the survey moved to the north side in October, researchers newly confirmed the remains of another building, where nearly square postholes about 1.7 meters on a side stood in a row at intervals of about 3 meters (10 shaku). The overall form of the building is still unknown, but it stands almost at the center of the precinct and is thought to be the precinct's Seiden (main hall). This shows that two buildings stood side by side within the precinct, one to the south and one to the north.

【The Same Standards as Heijo Palace — Likely the Saio's Palace】The size and spacing of the postholes and the north–south arrangement of the buildings share the characteristics of palace buildings confirmed at sites such as the Heijo Palace in Nara Prefecture, so the museum considers it highly likely that these buildings were the palace of the Saio. Earlier excavations at the Saiku Ruins have confirmed several rectangular walled precincts thought to be the Saio's palace from the Asuka through the Heian periods. Excavations at the Saiku Ruins have continued for more than 50 years.

An on-site briefing was held from 10:00 to 11:30 on Saturday, November 14, and more than 200 visitors reportedly attended. Admission was free, and museum staff explained the findings on the spot.

For exam study, the key point is the relationship between Saiku and the Saio. The Saio was an unmarried imperial princess who served Ise Jingu in place of the emperor, a duty that continued for about 660 years. Saiku is the remains of the palace where the Saio lived, and it is designated a national historic site. Ise Jingu and Saiku are linked through both the form of worship and the lives of the people who served it. See also the related article 'A 1,300-Year Cycle of Rebuilding Every 20 Years — Ise Jingu Begins Cutting Timber for Its 63rd Shikinen Sengu.'

When visiting the Saiku Ruins, please check the latest information in the official guidance of the Saiku History Museum. This site's event calendar also lists the '6th Shinens Kansha Ise Shinshu-sai,' held on November 23 to celebrate the Niinamesai festival of Ise Jingu.

A word from Suzu: A palace built to the capital's standards stood here too, for the Saio who served Ise. The rows of postholes let us see life from 1,300 years ago.
