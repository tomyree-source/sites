Gujo Hachiman Castle Maple Festival 2026: The White Keep Ablaze in Autumn Leaves, Opening October 31

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: an announcement by the Gujo Hachiman Industrial Promotion Corporation (an organizer's press release carried on PR TIMES and Jiji.com). The annual autumn event “Gujo Hachiman Castle Maple Festival 2026 — Tenshu Enjo” will be held from Saturday, October 31 to Sunday, November 29, 2026, at Gujo Hachiman Castle in Gujo City, Gifu Prefecture. This year's theme is “Tenshu Enjo — a sight only those who climb can meet.” You can climb to the castle on foot from the castle town in about 15 minutes.

The white keep wrapped in red and orange autumn leaves, the whole mountain dyed in autumn colors, the trees surrounding the keep shining a deep crimson — the organizers call this scenery “Tenshu Enjo” (“the keep ablaze”). It is a seasonal view you can encounter at the end of a walk up from the castle town.

From Friday, November 13 to Monday, November 23 (holiday), the “Tenshu Enjo Special Night Opening — Tenku no Momiji Teien (Sky Maple Garden) Illumination” will be held, lighting up the keep wrapped in autumn leaves and the “Sky Maple Garden” from sunset to 8:00 p.m. For the three days from Saturday, November 21 to Monday, November 23 (holiday), the “Illumination Special Nights — Wagasa Umbrella Collaboration” will stage the castle gate and the plaza in front of the keep with colorful Japanese umbrellas and lights. In addition, from Friday, November 13 to Sunday, November 15, a blue illumination in step with World Diabetes Day will also be held.

On Saturday, November 7, there will be the “Symphony of the Sky Castle — Tenshu Kyoso” (11:00 a.m.), with a performance by the Gujo Kita High School brass band played from the windows of the keep. On Sunday, November 15, the “Ogaki Matchlock Troop Demonstration” will be held in the plaza in front of the keep (11:00 a.m. and 1:30 p.m., about 30 minutes each). The limited-period “Goshuin Tour of the Successive Lords' Family Crests” is completed by touring the castle, collecting the family crest stamps on each floor, and stamping the golden crest of the Aoyama family, the last lordly house, on the top floor. Regular opening hours are 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. (last admission 4:45 p.m.), and admission is 400 yen for adults and 200 yen for children.

This connects with themes in castle exam study. First, “the experience of climbing to a mountain castle.” At a castle that makes use of mountain terrain, the act of climbing itself becomes a lesson in feeling how strong the defenses once were. Second, “how goshuin work.” Goshuin are distributed at castles as a memento of having climbed to them, and some, like this one, are project-style, in which you collect stamps as you tour the castle. Third, “how to enjoy the castle town and the castle mountain together.” Walking the castle town, climbing the castle mountain, and viewing the castle from both the foot and the summit reveals the relationship between castle and town.

Please note that events may be canceled in the event of rain, and dates and times may change without notice. Before you go, check the latest information on the special event website.

A word from Tsurugi: A white keep that looks as if it is ablaze in autumn leaves — a view truly worthy of its name. Climbing up from the castle town by day, and the illumination by night — the same castle seems to show a different face depending on the time of day.
