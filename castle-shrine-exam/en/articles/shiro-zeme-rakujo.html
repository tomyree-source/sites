Sieges and the Fall of Castles in the Sengoku Period: Starvation Sieges, Water Attacks and When a Castle Fell

Castles were built to defend, but in history many castles were attacked and fell. Learn the methods of castle assault in the Sengoku period, and the meaning of a castle's defensive devices becomes visible from the other side.

【Surround and Cut Off: The Starvation Siege】The most basic method of attack was the 'hyoro-zeme' (starvation siege, or siege by encirclement): surround the castle and cut its supply lines. Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Tottori Castle is known as an example of a starvation siege. The defenders endured on stores of rice and water, but when the stores ran out, the castle was opened without a fight. This is why wells and food stores were vital castle facilities.

【The Water Attack, Using the Terrain】When Hideyoshi attacked Bicchu Takamatsu Castle, he is said to have built embankments around it and drawn in river water to surround the castle with water — the famous 'mizu-zeme' (water attack). A water attack required large-scale earthworks to be carried out in a short time, a method that tested the attacking side's organizational power. The ability to read the terrain was essential both in building castles and in besieging them.

【Frontal Assault and Inducement】A frontal assault pressing straight at the moats and stone walls cost the attackers heavily too. For that reason, inducement — winning over generals inside the castle — and opening the castle through negotiation were also important means of castle attack. A castle's fall was not necessarily the result of battle.

【Exam Study Points】The textbook has no chapter devoted to sieges, but the mountain-castle defenses of Chapter 1 and the ingenuity of moats and tiger-mouth gates in Chapter 3 are all preparations against 'how will we be attacked.' Review the vocabulary of defense from the attacker's viewpoint and your understanding becomes three-dimensional.

When you visit a castle, look at the terrain with the feelings of both the defenders and the attackers. The depth of the moat and the bend of the gate will come to hold new meaning.
