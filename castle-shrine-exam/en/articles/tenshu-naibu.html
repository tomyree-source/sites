Inside the Castle Keep: Steep Stairs, Stone-Dropping Openings and the Secrets of Its Pillars

Another article introduced the types of keeps and how to tell them apart. This time we step further in and talk about the 'inside' of a keep. Climb a surviving keep and you will be surprised at how differently its space is made from a modern building.

【The Meaning of the Steep Stairs】The stairs of surviving keeps are astonishingly steep. This is sometimes explained as a device to make it hard for enemies to climb up, but structural necessity — fitting each floor into a limited floor area — is also said to have played a large part. The stairs are set in different positions on each floor rather than running straight to the top, another feature of the internal structure. Hold the handrail and climb one step at a time.

【Stone-Dropping Openings and Loopholes】In the floors along the walls of keeps and turrets there may be 'ishio-toshi' — openings that drop down toward the ground, a device for attacking enemies who had pressed up against the wall from directly above. The small holes opened in the walls, 'sama' (loopholes), were for shooting guns and bows, and their shapes are said to have been divided by use. At Matsumoto Castle and elsewhere, you can check the positions of stone-dropping openings and loopholes from inside.

【The Structure of Pillars and Beams】What supports a keep is the combination of thick pillars and beams. The great keep of Himeji Castle is known for its large central pillar, the shinbashira, but keep structures differ from castle to castle, and not all of them have a central pillar. How each structure withstood earthquakes and wind is a field where research continues castle by castle.

【Exam Study Points】This is the keeps section of Chapter 2 of the textbook. Together with the classification of exteriors (boro type and soto type), organize the interior defensive devices — stone-dropping openings and loopholes. The thinking is shared with Chapter 3's masugata and the defense of the castle as a whole.

When you climb a keep, look not only at the view but at your feet and the walls. The steepness of the stairs and the devices in the floor all tell the role this building played.
