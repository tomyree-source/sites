Castles and Hot Springs Go Well Together: Walking the Famous Baths Beside Famous Castles

When you put together the itinerary of a castle-touring trip, choosing a hot spring resort as your place to stay raises the satisfaction of the trip considerably. At the end of a day spent climbing stone walls and walking while looking up at a keep, you soak in a bath — castles and hot springs are, in fact, a very compatible combination. This time, we set out how to think about trips that combine famous castles with famous baths.

Matsuyama City in Ehime Prefecture is a representative example. Matsuyama Castle on Mt. Katsuyama is a famous castle with a surviving linked keep complex, and within the city is Dogo Onsen, one of Japan's three oldest hot springs. The flow of touring the castle by day and entering the waters of the Dogo Onsen Honkan in the evening is spoken of as a classic of Matsuyama travel. It is a fine example in which the castle exam's “twelve surviving original keeps” and the hot spring exam's “three oldest hot springs of Japan” overlap in a single trip.

If you trace how a hot spring resort came into being, you can see the history of the land. Arima Onsen is known for Toyotomi Hideyoshi's therapeutic bathing, and at Kinosaki Onsen, the culture of touring the public baths (sotoyu) has created the streetscape of the hot spring town. The history of the waters is connected to the history of transport and of daily life.

This is a theme spanning the castle exam and the hot spring exam. First, confirming the two lineups: “Matsuyama Castle is one of the twelve surviving original keeps, and Dogo Onsen is one of Japan's three oldest hot springs.” Second, “the distinction between the Three Famous Springs of Japan (Kusatsu, Gero, Arima) and the Three Oldest Hot Springs of Japan (Arima, Dogo, Shirahama).” Arima appears on both lists. Third, “the culture of communal baths.” The Dogo Onsen Honkan, Kinosaki's sotoyu, and Arima's Kin-no-yu and Gin-no-yu can be organized as examples of local communal baths that became the face of tourism.

On your next trip, try putting your itinerary together while moving back and forth between the castle guide pages and the hot spring introduction pages. The knowledge you learned for the exams is sure to change how the scenery at your destination looks.
