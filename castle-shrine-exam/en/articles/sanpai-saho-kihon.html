How to Pray at a Shrine: Temizu Purification and Two Bows, Two Claps, One Bow

Have you ever visited a shrine and, unsure of the order of worship, ended up copying the people around you? The basics of etiquette are simple once learned. Here we follow the standard shrine visiting etiquette in order, from the entrance.

Passing the torii and walking the approach — Bowing lightly before the torii and then passing through is considered polite etiquette. The center of the approach is called the 'seichu' and has been regarded as the deity's path, so worshippers customarily walk a little to the side. First purify your hands and mouth at the temizuya (purification basin), then proceed to the worship hall.

The temizu procedure — The steps are as follows. Take the ladle in your right hand and purify your left hand. Then switch the ladle to your left hand and purify your right hand. Switch back to your right hand, receive water in your left palm, and rinse your mouth. Finally, tilt the ladle upright so that water runs down its handle to purify it, then set it down face down. The first hand purified is the left — this is a point asked on the Shrine Exam.

Worship at the worship hall — Before the worship hall, put in your offering money quietly and ring the bell, then perform two bows, two claps, one bow. Bow deeply twice (the bows), clap twice at chest height (the claps), and bow deeply once more at the end (the final bow). Some shrines advise stating your address and name in your heart before making your wish.

Exceptions at some shrines — Etiquette also varies by shrine. At Izumo Taisha, for instance, worshippers are guided to worship with four claps; regional and shrine traditions shape their own forms. When unsure, the surest course is to follow the shrine's information signs.

Exam study points — A central theme of Chapter 3 of the Shrine Exam textbook. Two points are themselves exam questions: at temizu, 'the first hand purified is the left,' and worship is 'two bows, two claps, one bow.' Learn them tied to the actual flow of a visit.

Next time you visit a shrine, try worshipping with the etiquette in mind. When the sequence of gestures falls into place, even the way the air of the grounds feels will change.
