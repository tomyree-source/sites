Fushimi Inari Taisha and the Senbon Torii: Walking the Vermilion Gates of Mount Inari

Fushimi Inari Taisha, in Fushimi Ward, Kyoto, is the head shrine of the roughly 30,000 Inari shrines said to exist across Japan. The rows of vermilion torii gates running over the whole mountain are one of Japan's representative landscapes, drawing large numbers of worshippers from home and abroad.

【Inari Okami and the Enshrined Deities】The principal deity is Inari Okami, centered on Ukanomitama no Okami, enshrined as a god of rice, food and bountiful harvests. Originally worshipped as a god of abundant grain harvests, the deity came in time to be widely revered as a guardian of business prosperity and industry. The deities of Fushimi Inari Taisha are basic knowledge on the Shrine Certification exam as well.

【How the Senbon Torii Came to Be】The torii gates lining the approach have been dedicated with the wish that a prayer will 'pass through' (toru). The custom of dedicating torii is said to have spread in the Edo period, and today more than a thousand gates are said to stand in rows along the paths of Mount Inari. Look at the back of a gate and you will find the name of the person or company that dedicated it, carved together with the date.

【Walking Mount Inari】Mount Inari itself, rising behind the main hall, is an object of worship, and countless large and small otsuka — stones with the names of deities carved into mounds — are also enshrined across the mountain. From Yotsutsuji crossing you can look out over the city of Kyoto, and walking all the way to the Ichinomine summit makes a half-day pilgrimage around the mountain. Along the way is the famous 'omokaru ishi': you make a wish, then lift the top part of a stone lantern — if it feels lighter than you expected, your wish will come true; if heavier, more effort is needed.

【Foxes Are the Messengers of Inari】The foxes seated here and there in the precincts are regarded as the messengers (kenzoku) of Inari Okami. They are shown holding a key, a ball, a scroll or an ear of rice in their mouths, and their existence serves a different role from that of komainu. Hunting for the differences in the foxes' expressions as you walk is one of the pleasures of Fushimi Inari.

【Exam Study Points】This is covered in Chapter 5 of the Shrine Certification textbook. Fix two points for certain: 'the principal deity of Fushimi Inari Taisha is Ukanomitama no Okami' and 'it is the head shrine of the Inari shrines.' Understand also, as culture, that the Senbon Torii is an accumulation of dedications.

At Fushimi Inari Taisha, walking to the summit shows you an entirely different view. Give yourself plenty of time and take on the full circuit of Mount Inari.
