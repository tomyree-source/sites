A Nara-Period Timber Fence Beneath a Heian Wall — Taga Castle Ruins Yield a Clue to Its Changing Ramparts

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: NEWSjp (November 12, 2026). At Taga Castle ruins, a nationally designated Special Historic Site, the excavation was opened to the press on November 12. The survey has turned up an important clue to how the ramparts changed over time.

Taga Castle ruins were the political, cultural, and military center of ancient Tohoku. The site is thought to have once been enclosed by ramparts about 900 meters square. Since May this year, the prefecture has been excavating the rampart at the site's northwest corner.

What the survey found was a 'timber fence' (zaimoku-bei) from an older period than the 'rammed-earth wall' (tsuiji-bei) — a wall made by pressing earth solid. The rammed-earth wall is attributed to the Heian period, but beneath it lay a fence made of logs laid side by side. This timber fence may have been built in the Nara period, and it is hoped it will shed light on what the ramparts really looked like. According to the Kahoku Shimpo (November 13, 2026), the survey covered an area about 30 meters north-south by 10 meters east-west around the northwest corner, and six leveled layers and rammed-earth walls were confirmed along the north and west sides. The rammed-earth wall passed through three phases, rebuilt twice while shifting slightly inward at nearly the same position.

The prefecture was reported to be holding a public on-site briefing at 10:30 a.m. on November 14 to present the survey's findings. Kazuya Hirotani, deputy chief researcher at the Miyagi Taga Castle Ruins Research Institute, which conducted the survey, said, 'What has survived here since ancient times is a very important treasure, so I hope people will come and see the traces of Taga Castle up close.'

For exam study, the key point is Taga Castle and the role of ancient castle stockades. In 724, in the Nara period, Taga Castle became the seat of the Mutsu provincial government and the Chinju-fu military headquarters — the political and military center of the region. The government compound enclosed by rammed-earth walls has been excavated, and its main hall and other buildings have been reconstructed. As the center of the ancient administration of Tohoku, the site is designated a Special Historic Site. Reading the changes of an era from the types of ramparts and how they overlap is the very joy of excavation.
