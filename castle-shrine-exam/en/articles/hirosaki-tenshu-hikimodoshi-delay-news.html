Hirosaki Castle: Seating of the Keep in the “Hikimodoshi” Return Postponed to Around Autumn 2027

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Jiji Press (October 21, 2026). At Hirosaki Castle in Hirosaki City, Aomori Prefecture, in the “hikimodoshi” work to return the keep to its original position, the timing for seating the keep on the keep base whose repairs have been completed has been postponed from the original plan of mid-November 2026 to around autumn 2027.

Originally, the keep was scheduled to be seated during November 2026 on the keep base whose repairs had been completed. However, corrosion affecting the move was found in the keep, so the seating has been postponed to around autumn 2027. In construction work that moves a building handed down since the Edo period, what is required above all is to proceed carefully while checking its condition.

The keep of Hirosaki Castle is designated an Important Cultural Property and is one of the twelve keeps surviving in Japan today. In 2015, work was carried out to move this keep — the entire 400-ton building, just as it was — about 78 meters, in order to repair the stone walls of the keep base. It is an approach that advances the repairs while preserving its form as a cultural property, by moving the building without dismantling it and fixing the foundation's stone walls. With the stone wall repairs finished, the “hikimodoshi” to return the keep to its original position has been under way since July 2026.

After it is seated, the keep is scheduled to be enclosed in a protective shelter (oiyane) for preservation repair. For that reason, the keep's exterior will not be viewable until around 2033, when the work ends. During this period, Hirosaki City says it will convey the state of the keep through such means as publishing videos and holding on-site tours. Precisely because there will be a long period when its figure cannot be seen, the effort to convey the construction process itself becomes important.

This connects with Chapter 4 of the castle exam textbook. First, the “twelve surviving original keeps.” Hirosaki Castle's keep is one of the twelve keeps built in or before the Edo period that survive today. Second, “Important Cultural Properties.” Work to repair a building designated by the national government as a cultural property requires procedures that protect its form and value. Third, “the preservation-repair concept of moving a keep to repair its stone walls (hikiya, hikimodoshi).” Keep in mind, together with the article on Maruoka Castle's preservation repair, that there is a method of moving the building itself in order to repair its foundation.

The seating is expected around autumn 2027. After that, it will be enclosed in a protective shelter, and its exterior will not be viewable until around 2033. If you have an opportunity to visit Hirosaki Castle, turn your eyes also to the appearance of the keep just for now — in the middle of its journey back to its original position.
