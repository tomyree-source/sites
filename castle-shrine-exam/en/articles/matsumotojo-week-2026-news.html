Matsumoto Castle Week Brings a French Castle to Town: First Collaboration with Chambord for “Matsumoto Castle Day”

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Sources: the Chunichi Shimbun (November 1, 2026) and the official Matsumoto City tourism site “Visit Matsumoto.” In Matsumoto City, Nagano Prefecture, “National Treasure Matsumoto Castle Week” is being held from Tuesday, November 3 (a national holiday) to Tuesday, November 10, timed to “Matsumoto Castle Day” on November 10. The highlight is a collaboration exhibition with Chambord Castle, a World Heritage site in France, with which Matsumoto Castle concluded a sister-castle agreement in April this year. The exhibition is being held for the first time at the Taikomon Gate, which is specially opened during the period.

The Taikomon Gate, the venue for the exhibition, is specially opened during the period. Chambord Castle is a World Heritage site in France, and it concluded a sister-castle agreement with Matsumoto Castle in April 2026. The first event between two castles across the sea is being held in the castle grounds, with the National Treasure keep looking on.

The events of “National Treasure Matsumoto Castle Week,” as listed by the official Matsumoto City tourism site, are a chrysanthemum exhibition, the special opening of the Taikomon Gate, an autumn tea ceremony, the Nijurokuya-shin Annual Festival, a performance by the Kojō Taiko drummers, sales of Matsumoto Castle Week limited-edition goods, a panel exhibition marking the 150th anniversary of the former Kaichi School building, and a Hōsha Kyūdō (ceremonial archery) tournament. Matsumoto City and Himeji City mark the 60th anniversary of their sister-city agreement in 2026, and from November 1 to 30, residents of Himeji can enter National Treasure Matsumoto Castle and other designated facilities free of charge (official Matsumoto City tourism site, published October 23, 2026).

Matsumoto Castle is one of the twelve surviving original keeps, and its keep is a National Treasure. Its black-lacquered exterior has earned it the nickname “Crow Castle” (Karasu-jō). The keep is a linked compound structure consisting of the main keep, the Inui Small Keep, the Watari-yagura turret, the Tatsumi-tsuke-yagura turret, and the Tsukimi-yagura turret, in a five-story, six-floor layout. For the exams, remember “National Treasure,” “Crow Castle,” “five stories, six floors,” and “Ishikawa Kazumasa and Yasunaga.” Like the sister-castle agreement, the connections between castles are also worth remembering along with their castle towns. See also the related article “Matsumoto Castle, the Crow Castle: The Sengoku Tension Spoken by Its Black Keep.”

“National Treasure Matsumoto Castle Week” runs until Tuesday, November 10. Many of the events, including the special opening of the Taikomon Gate and the exhibition, are limited to this period, so please check the latest information on the official Matsumoto City tourism site before you go. This site's events calendar also lists the National Treasure Matsumoto Castle Chrysanthemum Exhibition (October 29–November 13) and the Matsumoto Castle Projection Mapping (December 12–February 14, 2027).

A word from Tsurugi: The first collaboration with Chambord Castle, all the way from across the sea. With the special opening of the Taikomon Gate on top of that, Matsumoto Castle's autumn is packed with highlights. “Matsumoto Castle Day” on November 10 is just around the corner.
