Himeji's First Gold-Leaf Roof Tile Goes on Show — 'TOYOTOMI IMPACT' Exhibition Runs to December 20

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Sources: Kobe Shimbun NEXT (November 7, 2026) and the official guide of the Himeji City Buried Cultural Properties Center. The autumn special exhibition “TOYOTOMI IMPACT — New Horizons in Research on Himeji Castle in the Hashiba and Kinoshita Periods” is now open at the Himeji City Buried Cultural Properties Center (Shiko-cho Sakamoto, Himeji City, Hyogo Prefecture). The exhibition explores Himeji Castle and its castle town as ruled by the Toyotomi clan, led by Hashiba Hideyoshi, and was organized to coincide with the NHK Taiga drama “Toyotomi Kyodai!” — whose central figure is Hideyoshi's younger brother, Hidenaga. It runs from Sunday, October 4 to Sunday, December 20, 2026.

The highlight is a gold-leaf roof tile — the first ever found in Hyogo Prefecture — now on public display for the first time. It was excavated in the 2025 fiscal-year survey from the castle-town remains southeast of Harima-no-Kuni Sosha Shrine (Sosha-Honmachi, Himeji City): the lower half of a tile, with a trace of gold leaf confirmed under an optical microscope. Gold-leaf tiles are regarded as a symbol of power, but as Kobe Shimbun notes, the tile was found together with pottery from the end of the Edo period, so it cannot be confirmed as a tile from Hideyoshi's time as lord of the castle. The exhibition presents 177 excavated items, including the gold-leaf tile.

Tiles found during the great Showa-era restoration (1956–1964) could be identified as six tiles from Hideyoshi's castle-building period, thanks to records of the depth of the strata in which they were found. The “Hideyoshi tiles” are thought to have been fired in quantity rather than for quality, at Hideyoshi's direction. The exhibition also introduces the structure of the stone walls of Hideyoshi's period, and a catalogue (600 yen including tax) explains the research findings in detail.

For the castle exams, remember that Himeji Castle is a National Treasure and a World Heritage site with a surviving original keep. Hashiba Hideyoshi built the castle in 1580 (Tensho 8), and Ikeda Terumasa later carried out a major rebuilding that created the white-walled keep complex seen today. Excavated items such as tiles and stone walls are also important clues for understanding a castle's history — the archaeological view matters too. See also the related article “Himeji Castle: 400 Years of History — How It Survived War to Become a World Heritage Site.”

The exhibition runs until Sunday, December 20, 2026, from 10:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. (last entry 4:30 p.m.), and admission is free. The center is closed every Monday (open on public holidays) and on the day after a public holiday (open on Saturdays and Sundays). Exhibition talks are held on Saturday, November 14 and Saturday, December 19 (free, no reservation needed). Before you go, please check the latest information on the Himeji City Buried Cultural Properties Center's official website. This site's events calendar also lists the exhibition dates.
