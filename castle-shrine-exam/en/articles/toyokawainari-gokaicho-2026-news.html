Toyokawa Inari in Aichi: The First Gokaicho in 72 Years Begins — Special Opening of the Secret Image from November 1 to 23

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: a press release by the religious corporation Myogonji (October 24, 2026, carried on PR TIMES). At Toyokawa Inari (Enpukuzan Myogonji, 1 Toyokawa-cho, Toyokawa City) in Toyokawa City, Aichi Prefecture, a gokaicho will be held from Sunday, November 1 to Monday, November 23, 2026 (a national holiday) — the first in 72 years, since 1954. Although Toyokawa Inari is fondly known by the name “Inari,” it is formally a temple of the Soto school of Zen, Myogonji. For shrine exam study, this is a topic to keep in mind as a milestone of faith passed down across shrines and temples alike, together with the spread of Inari worship.

Ahead of the gokaicho, on Saturday, October 24, the Goenbashira pillar that symbolizes the gokaicho was erected in front of the main hall. The Goenbashira is a square stupa-like pillar set up in front of the main hall at the time of a gokaicho, and is said to praise the virtue of the deity and pray for the fulfillment of worshippers' various wishes. The secret image, Toyokawa Dakini Shinten, is connected — through the front-standing image that serves as its substitute, and the front-standing image before that — by five-colored “Zen-no-tsuna” cords to the Goenbashira in front of the Great Main Hall. During the gokaicho period, worshippers are said to be able to form a bond (kechien) with the deity by touching the pillar, with the same merit as touching the deity directly.

The main related events are as follows. On Saturday, October 31, the Shinden door-opening ceremony, opening pilgrimage and bond-connecting ceremony; on Sunday, November 1, the gokaicho begins; on Saturday, November 21 and Sunday, November 22, the Gokaicho Grand Festival; and on Monday, November 23 (holiday), the Shinden door-closing ceremony. Within the 23-day period, the opening of the doors, the grand festival and the closing of the doors are each milestone days. When you visit, please check the latest announcements on the official gokaicho website.

This connects with themes in shrine exam and travel study. First, “what is a gokaicho?” A gokaicho is an event in which a secret image, normally not open to view, is specially shown to the public for a limited period. Second, “the spread of Inari worship.” The Inari deity has been widely worshipped at shrines and temples in many places as a guardian of food, business and daily life. Third, “the milestone of the Year of the Horse.” This gokaicho falls in a Year of the Horse, and the previous one, in 1954, was also a Year of the Horse. The interval of 72 years itself speaks to the preciousness of this event.

The period runs until November 23. Crowds are also expected on November 21 and 22, when the Gokaicho Grand Festival is held. Before you go, check the schedule and how to worship in the official announcements.

A word from Suzu: The weight of the words “first in 72 years” — the arrangement of touching the Goenbashira and wishing to form a bond is an experience in which you can feel the very form of faith. A trip tracing the milestone days, from the opening of the doors to their closing, sounds enjoyable too.
