Maruoka Castle Livestreams Its Keep Restoration: About 90 People Attend the Briefing

This is a news report on the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. The source is the Chunichi Shimbun (October 26, 2026). A briefing on the preservation and repair work being carried out on the keep of Maruoka Castle in Sakai City, Fukui Prefecture, was held on October 24, 2026, and the state of the worksite was shown by livestream.

Inside the construction screens, by livestream — The briefing was held at the Maruoka Castle Machiyori Market, the castle's tourist information center in the city, and was hosted by the Sakai City Board of Education. Through the livestream, participants viewed the site inside the construction screens — the dismantled roof and walls, and features such as the shachihoko roof ornaments — and confirmed how the work was progressing. The broadcast also showed walls after their plaster had been taken apart.

About 90 participants, some standing — Around 90 people took part in the briefing, and the livestream was held three times in total. Interest ran so high that some attendees had to stand; the contractor's site supervisor reported live from the worksite, and participants followed the situation on monitors inside the center. It is clear that the sight of a keep mid-repair — something normally never seen — drew great interest.

Work runs to November 2027, with seismic measures too — The preservation and repair work is scheduled to run from March 2026 to November 2027. It will repair age-deteriorated parts such as the roof and walls, and carry out seismic reinforcement and other measures. Maruoka Castle's keep is one of the surviving original keeps studied in the exam textbooks. To protect an old building for the long term, both repairing damaged parts and adding preparedness against earthquakes are indispensable.

Exam study points — Themes tied to Chapters 4 and 5 of the Castle Exam textbook. First, what is repaired in the preservation of a surviving keep: mainly age-deteriorated areas such as roofs and walls, with the principle of intervening while preserving the building's appearance as a cultural property. Second, ingenuity in showing work in progress: showing a site hidden behind screens by livestream keeps people's interest connected even during the construction period. Third, the meaning of seismic measures: in earthquake-prone Japan, protecting historic wooden buildings requires preparedness against earthquakes alongside repair of deterioration — organize your notes from that viewpoint.

The work is scheduled to continue until November 2027. While waiting for the day the restored keep reappears, do look out for chances like this one to turn your eyes to 'Maruoka Castle as it is being repaired right now.'
