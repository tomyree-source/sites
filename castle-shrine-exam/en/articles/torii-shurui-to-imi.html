Torii Gate Types and Meanings: Shinmei Torii, Myojin Torii, and the Parts Kasagi, Shimaki and Nuki

The torii is the face of a shrine and a structure symbolizing the Japanese landscape. Red torii, stone torii, torii standing in the sea. Their shapes fall into two great lineages, and each part has its own name. Learn to tell them apart, and shrine touring gains a level of resolution.

The role of the torii — A torii is a gate marking the boundary (kekkai) between the sacred precinct where the deity resides and the everyday world where people live. The first gate on the approach is counted the ichi-no-torii, the next the ni-no-torii; at a large shrine you pass through several torii on your way to the worship hall. Bowing as you pass through is etiquette expressing awareness of that boundary.

Learn the names of the parts — The topmost crossbeam of a torii is the 'kasagi.' Below it, the beam supporting the kasagi is the 'shimaki'; the crossbeam passing through the pillars is the 'nuki'; and the short central post joining kasagi and nuki is the 'gakuzuka.' These names are asked on the Shrine Exam. Begin by looking for the kasagi at the very top.

Shinmei torii and myojin torii — Torii shapes divide broadly into 'shinmei torii' and 'myojin torii.' The shinmei torii is straight and plain overall; the Uji Bridge torii of Ise Jingu is the representative example. The myojin torii is an elegant form whose kasagi curves upward at both ends and whose pillars incline slightly inward, seen at shrines all over Japan. Telling these two apart is the first basic.

Differences of color and material — Vermilion torii are familiar from Inari shrines; vermilion is said to carry the meaning of warding off evil. Materials also vary — plain wood, stone, bronze — and some, like the great torii standing in the sea at Itsukushima Shrine, are landmarks in themselves by their very location.

Exam study points — Within the scope of Chapter 3 of the Shrine Exam textbook. The parts knowledge — 'the topmost crossbeam is the kasagi' — is frequently asked. Also organize the lineages: shinmei torii equals the Ise type; myojin torii equals the curved form.

Next time you visit a shrine, stop once before the torii and check the curve of the kasagi and the incline of the pillars. The familiar torii will look a little different.
