A 2.5-Kilometre Corridor of Light: Himeji Castle and Its Castle Town Glow with 510,000 LEDs

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Kobe Shimbun NEXT (November 24, 2026). An illumination colouring the centre of Himeji City in winter has begun. This year the area has expanded, and a 2.5-kilometre route is linked by 510,000 light-emitting diodes (LEDs). The city says the 'corridor of light' expresses 'a fantastical and special night.'

【A 2.5-Kilometre Route of 510,000 Lights】The route the city recommends starts at JR Himeji Station, heads north along Otemae-dori beneath street trees whose bulbs change colour with the time, then turns left at the intersection in front of Himeji Castle, follows the moat through Himeyama Park clockwise, passes the north side of the main keep, and enters Sannomaru Square. The whole route was supervised by the world-renowned lighting designer Motoko Ishii. Including the surrounding events, the city aims for 800,000 visitors during the period. Admission is free, and the lights are on from 5:00 p.m. to 10:00 p.m.

【The Sea of Clouds at Sakuramon Bridge and the Water Mirror in Sannomaru Square】One highlight is the Sakuramon Bridge over the inner moat. Mist is sprayed from beneath the bridge and lit with blue-toned light, so the great keep of Himeji Castle appears to float on a sea of clouds. The path along the moat is named the 'Atara-yo Mawari-michi,' and offers various light features, including the autumn-leaf illumination and projection mapping in Himeyama Park. Entering Sannomaru Square, you are met by a light display titled 'Atara-yo no Yume-kagami' (Dream Mirror of the Atara Night). In a water mirror 20 metres wide and 10 metres deep, evoking the waterside of a garden said to have once stood there, the great keep is reflected upside down, and images of the four seasons — fireworks, snow and more — are projected on the water.

【For Exam Study】For exam study, the key points are the 'whiteness' of Himeji Castle and its value as a World Heritage site. Himeji Castle was built by Ikeda Terumasa between 1601 and 1609, and its white plastered walls give it the name 'White Heron Castle' (Hakurojo). In December 1993, together with Horyu-ji Temple, it became Japan's first World Heritage site. This illumination is an attempt to link the castle and its castle town in a single corridor. See also the related article 'Himeji Castle: 400 Years — How It Survived War and Became a World Heritage Site.'

The illumination runs until January 11, 2027. Our events calendar also lists the 'Himeji Otemae-dori Illumination.'

A word from Tsurugi: 2.5 kilometres of light — just walking it feels like stepping inside the castle's history. The sea of clouds at Sakuramon Bridge, and the water mirror in Sannomaru Square. The White Heron Castle reflected upside down — not to be missed.
