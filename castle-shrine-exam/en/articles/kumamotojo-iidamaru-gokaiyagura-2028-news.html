Built, Taken Apart, and Built Again — Kumamoto Castle's Iidamaru Five-Story Turret Heads Back to Restoration

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Livedoor News (KKT Kumamoto Kenmin Television, November 12, 2026). At Kumamoto Castle, the stone wall supporting the Iidamaru Five-Story Turret has been rebuilt, and restoration work is about to begin again. Completion is expected in fiscal 2028.

The restoration is led by master carpenter Nobuhiro Saruwatari (66), a native of Nagasaki Prefecture who lives in Kumamoto City. A veteran with 50 years in the trade, his connection with Kumamoto Castle goes back to 2000. At the time, the castle was restoring various turrets under the 'Heisei Restoration Project,' and Saruwatari worked on the Hitsujisaru Turret (completed 2003), the Iidamaru Five-Story Turret (completed 2005), and the Honmaru Goten palace (completed 2008).

In the 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes, every building at Kumamoto Castle was damaged — all 13 nationally designated Important Cultural Properties and all 20 reconstructed or restored buildings. The Iidamaru Five-Story Turret was held up and kept from collapsing by what became known as the 'miraculous single stone wall,' a symbol of the quake damage. The turret is not itself a designated cultural property, but it was built using historical methods, and because the building survived without collapsing, its parts — including the shiguchi joints where timbers are fitted together — escaped damage, making restoration easier.

Afterward, Saruwatari and his fellow craftsmen dismantled the Iidamaru Five-Story Turret by hand. Now the stone wall supporting the building has been rebuilt, and restoration work is about to begin again. Saruwatari built the turret, took it apart with his own hands, and will now build it again. During the Kumamoto earthquake of July 2026, he was at the restoration site of five nationally designated Important Cultural Properties, including the Tagoto Turret, and saw a nearby stone wall collapse before his eyes. Full restoration of Kumamoto Castle as a whole is scheduled for fiscal 2052 (as of November 10).

For exam study, the key point is how Kumamoto Castle's restoration is carried out. Kumamoto Castle was built in 1607 by Kato Kiyomasa and is known for the keep rising above its stone walls and for the 'musha-gaeshi' stone walls, too steep for attackers to climb. The keep was also damaged in the 2016 earthquakes but was restored and reopened in 2021. For a building made with historical methods, like the Iidamaru Five-Story Turret, the process is to dismantle it while preserving its parts, rebuild the stone wall, and then build it again. See also the related article 'Kumamoto Castle and the Road to Recovery — From the Earthquakes to the Keep's Restoration, and the Long Path to Full Recovery.'

The Iidamaru Five-Story Turret is expected to be completed in fiscal 2028. When visiting Kumamoto Castle, please check the latest open areas in the castle's official guidance.
