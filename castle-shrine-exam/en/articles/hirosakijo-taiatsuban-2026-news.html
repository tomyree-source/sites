Hirosaki Castle: The 210-Ton Bearing Slab Is Fixed in Place, Ready to Receive the Keep

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: NTV News NNN (October 27, 2026, reporting by Aomori Broadcasting). At Hirosaki Castle in Hirosaki City, Aomori Prefecture, seismic work in step with the “hikimodoshi” — the return of the keep to its original location — has progressed, and the preparation of the “taiatsuban” (bearing slab), the foundation that supports the keep's weight evenly, is now complete. This blog has also reported on how the seating timing was revised to around autumn 2027; this article brings the subsequent progress: the news that the foundation side is now ready.

The work carried out on this day was to lower a reinforced-concrete bearing slab weighing 210 tons onto four piles driven into the ground and fix it in place. Using jacks, it was lowered 1.2 meters, proceeding carefully so as not to break its level, and after four hours the preparations to place the keep on it were complete. An official from Hirosaki City's Parks and Greenery Section explained that, for the time being, this completes the seismic reinforcement of the keep's foundation.

The move of the keep is expected to end next March, with a break during the snowy season. After that, preservation and repair work will begin, including seismic reinforcement of the keep itself, and the plan is to seat the keep on the bearing slab prepared this time by around autumn. In other words, now that the foundation is ready, the stage advances to work on the main body, after the keep has returned.

This connects with themes in castle exam study. First, the “taiatsuban” (bearing slab): the foundation for supporting the keep's weight evenly, prepared as part of the seismic reinforcement of the base so that it can withstand major earthquakes. Second, “hikimodoshi”: the construction work of moving the keep in order to return it to its original location. Third, “preservation repair and seismic reinforcement”: repairs to protect the keep itself and preparations against earthquakes are advanced as one. Hirosaki Castle is one of the twelve surviving original keeps and is also a National Important Cultural Property; keep in mind the whole sequence — the re-stacking of the stone walls and the moving and repair of the keep — as an effort to protect the castle.

A castle whose keep moves, a castle rebuilt from its foundation — Hirosaki Castle's “great renovation of the century” is still in progress. By following the flow through to the seating, you can see the procedures for passing a castle down to future generations. When you visit, check the latest announcements from Hirosaki City and Hirosaki Park on how to view the site before you go.
