Getting Started with Dual-Base Living: Turning a Favorite Travel Destination into a Second Home, Starting with a Trial Stay

Have you ever stood in a castle town or a hot-spring street on a trip and thought, "What would it be like to live here?" One way to turn that feeling into reality, without giving up your current home, is dual-base living: keeping a base in the city and another in a regional area, and traveling back and forth between them. This site has introduced castles, shrines, hot springs and sake regions as travel destinations; this new corner, Relocation & Living, adds the perspective of staying in those places as part of everyday life.

Relocation and dual-base living differ in where your main base sits. Relocation means moving the center of your life — your registered address and daily base — to a new place. Dual-base living keeps your current home and adds a second base that you visit regularly. Because you do not have to cut your ties to where you live now, it is a lighter first step, and it also works as a long rehearsal before an eventual move.

The first step is gathering information. Many municipalities run relocation pages and consultation desks, and relocation fairs and consultation events are held in major cities, where you can talk directly with municipal staff and people who have already moved. Compare several candidate areas rather than fixing on one, and note what each offers in daily-life terms: shopping, medical care, transport and climate.

The second step is a trial stay. Many municipalities offer trial-relocation houses or short-stay programs, and you can also use monthly rentals or a longer hotel stay. Even a few days spent buying groceries, riding local transport and walking the town in the morning and evening tells you far more than any brochure. Book the lodging part the way you would for a trip, and spend the days as if you lived there.

The third step is checking work and housing. Confirm whether your job allows remote work, look into local job information if you plan to work locally, and use the vacant-house banks (akiya banks) that many municipalities run to see what homes are available. Support schemes — for trial stays, moving costs or home renovation — differ greatly in content and conditions from one municipality to another, so always check the official information of the specific municipality you are considering rather than relying on summaries.

One concrete example: Yonago in Tottori Prefecture. The ruins of Yonago Castle, a designated national historic site, stand on a hill above the city, and from the keep platform you can look out to Mt. Daisen. Around October 22 and February 20 each year, when the weather cooperates, the rising sun sits right on Daisen's summit — a sight known as Diamond Daisen, for which viewing events are held. A place you first visit for a view like that can become a town you return to with the seasons; that is exactly the kind of connection dual-base living is good at growing.

You do not have to decide everything at once. The comfortable order is: travel, then a trial stay, then dual-base living, and only then — if it fits — relocation. In this corner we will keep adding practical pieces that connect the castles, hot springs and towns introduced on this site with the idea of living there, region by region and theme by theme.
