Shinto Shrine Architecture Styles: Telling Shinmei-zukuri, Taisha-zukuri and Nagare-zukuri Apart

The main hall (honden) of a shrine stands behind the worship hall and usually cannot be seen well. Yet the form of this honden carries the names of styles passed down from ancient times. Once you know the styles, you can imagine even an invisible honden from the shape of its roof.

【Shinmei-zukuri — The Form of Ise Jingu】Shinmei-zukuri is regarded as one of the oldest styles. It is a raised-floor building on posts dug into the ground, gabled and entered from the long side of the roof (hira-iri); at both ends of the roof the 'chigi' cross, and along the ridge the 'katsuo-gi' stand in a row. The main sanctuaries of Ise Jingu are the representative example, and the style is explained as preserving the form of ancient granaries. Its restrained, straight-lined beauty is its hallmark.

【Taisha-zukuri — The Form of Izumo Taisha】Taisha-zukuri is also an old style, characterized by a large gabled roof entered from the gable side (tsuma-iri). The main hall of Izumo Taisha is the representative example and is designated a National Treasure. That the position of the entrance differs from shinmei-zukuri is the first step in telling them apart. The style is also known for the thick pillar (shin-no-mihashira) standing inside.

【Nagare-zukuri — The Form Found All Over Japan】In nagare-zukuri, the front roof is extended in a long flowing sweep, and the space beneath it serves as a covered front porch (gohai). The Kamo shrines (Kamigamo Jinja and Shimogamo Jinja) and others are given as representative examples, and the style has been adopted by a great many shrines across Japan — it is the most widespread style of all. There are also styles named for individual shrines, such as the kasuga-zukuri of Kasuga Taisha.

【Later Styles, Such as Gongen-zukuri】Gongen-zukuri, represented by Nikko Toshogu of the Edo period, is a composite form connecting the honden and the worship hall with a low building called the 'ishi-no-ma,' and it is rich in decoration such as carving. In contrast to the plain, straight-lined old styles, styles tend to grow more complex and decorative as the ages advance — interesting to view alongside the changes in castle keeps and stone walls.

【Exam Study Points】This is a central theme of Chapter 2 of the Shrine Certification textbook. Three points appear on the exam just as they are: 'shinmei-zukuri = Ise Jingu,' 'taisha-zukuri = Izumo Taisha,' and 'chigi and katsuo-gi are ornaments of the honden roof.' Organize nagare-zukuri and gongen-zukuri together with their representative shrines as well.

On your next visit, look at the roof of the honden over the worship hall. If you can see chigi and katsuo-gi, that is a clue that the honden is in an old style.
