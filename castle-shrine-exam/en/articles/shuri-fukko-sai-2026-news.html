Shurijo Castle Reconstruction Festival, November 1–3: The Ryukyu Kingdom Picture Scroll Procession, Just Before the Seiden Opens to the Public

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Okinawa Television (October 29, 2026). On this day, the executive committee of the Shurijo Castle Reconstruction Festival held a press conference and called for people to attend the festival. The festival will be held for three days, from November 1 to November 3, 2026, at Shurijo Castle Park and Kokusai-dori in Naha City. Held just before the general opening of the restored Seiden begins on November 23, it is an opportunity to enjoy the figure of Shurijo Castle walking toward reconstruction, together with its processions.

On the first day, November 1, the “Ryukyu Kingdom Picture Scroll Procession” will be held on Kokusai-dori. The number of participants is about 900, a larger scale than in 2025. Shurijo Castle was the residence of the kings of the Ryukyu Kingdom, which lasted about 450 years, and a place that was the center of politics, diplomacy and religious ceremonies. The sight of a procession recreating the attire of that kingdom's era advancing down Naha's main street is a highlight unique to this festival.

On the final day, November 3, the “Koshiki Procession” will be held, and the king and queen will wear Ryukyu costumes that were lost in the 2019 fire and have since been restored. At Shurijo Castle, the Seiden and other buildings burned down in the fire of October 31, 2019, but restoration has been advanced not only of the buildings, but also of culture itself, such as the costumes. A procession in costumes revived after overcoming the fire is likely to convey the steps of reconstruction in visible form.

Related events are also scheduled after the festival. On November 28, a “Lantern Walk” heading to Shurijo Castle is scheduled to be held, with an application deadline of November 10. If you are considering taking part, please be careful of the deadline. Together with the Seiden's general opening (November 23), events continue in Shuri throughout November.

This connects with themes in castle exam study. First, “Shurijo Castle was a gusuku and a royal palace.” Shurijo Castle is one of Japan's 100 famous castles, and forms part of the World Heritage site “Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu.” Second, “Ryukyu Kingdom diplomacy and the role of Shurijo Castle.” The kingdom was bound to Ming and Qing China by tributary trade, and Shurijo Castle was also a stage of diplomacy that received envoys hospitably. Third, “restoration is not only about buildings.” Keep it in mind as an effort to convey culture, including costumes and the forms of ceremony, as in this Koshiki Procession.

The Shurijo Castle Reconstruction Festival runs for three days from November 1. Just before the Seiden's general opening, it is likely to be an opportunity to feel the steps of reconstruction along with the splendor of the processions. If you are planning to visit Shurijo Castle, please check the festival dates as well.
