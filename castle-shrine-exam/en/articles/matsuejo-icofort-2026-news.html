Matsue Hosts Castle Scholars from Around the World — Five National Treasure Keeps Aim for World Heritage as the 'Early Modern Castle Keeps'

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: the Asahi Shimbun (November 17, 2026) and the official site of the ICOFORT International Conference 2026 Matsue. From Sunday, November 8 to Wednesday, November 11, the International Conference of ICOFORT (the ICOMOS International Scientific Committee on Fortifications and Military Heritage), an academic committee of ICOMOS (the International Council on Monuments and Sites), the advisory body to UNESCO (the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization), was held in Matsue City, Shimane Prefecture. The venue was Matsue Terrsa, next to JR Matsue Station, and about 60 researchers from Japan and abroad gathered.

【Castles and Keeps — Defensive Systems, or Symbols?】The conference's overall theme was 'Castles, Fortifications and Tenshu — Defensive Systems or Symbols?' On the first day, November 8, a commemorative symposium titled 'The Age of Discovery and Castle Towns' was held. Milagros Flores Román, chair of ICOFORT, said that from the perspective of the 'heritage ecosystem,' which evaluates cultural heritage through its diverse relationships with nature, society, and the economy, 'Matsue is fascinating.' Among the Japanese researchers, Professor Emeritus Hitoshi Nakai of the University of Shiga Prefecture gave a lecture on 'The Keicho Castle-Building Rush and Matsue Castle' in the early Edo period, explaining that Matsue Castle, built together with four branch castles including Mitoya Castle to defend Izumo Province amid rising military tension between the Tokugawa shogunate and the Toyotomi family, 'can be positioned as a typical example of the Keicho castle-building rush.' Researchers from Spain, Iran, India, and South Korea also presented on the origins of fortifications in their own countries.

【Five National Treasure Keeps as the 'Early Modern Castle Keeps' for World Heritage】The conference also featured a report on efforts to seek inscription of the five National Treasure keeps — Matsue, Inuyama, Matsumoto, Hikone, and Himeji castles — as the 'Early Modern Castle Keeps' on the World Heritage list. The report was given by the preparatory council formed by the three cities of Matsue, Inuyama in Aichi Prefecture, and Matsumoto in Nagano Prefecture, which is advancing research positioning the five National Treasure keeps as the material evidence for the bid. On Matsue Castle, the Matsue Castle Research Committee of Matsue City presented a paper titled 'Matsue Castle as a Main Castle — From a Castle Built with Actual Combat in Mind to a Castle as a Symbol.' Japan's early modern castles are drawing attention as subjects for protection and research with World Heritage inscription in view, alongside the walled cities of China's Ming and Qing periods and the capital fortifications of Korea's Joseon period, built around the same time.

【For Exam Study】For exam study, the key point is the five National Treasure keeps and the effort to inscribe the 'Early Modern Castle Keeps' on the World Heritage list. The five keeps designated as National Treasures are those of Matsue, Inuyama, Matsumoto, Hikone, and Himeji castles. Hikone Castle was selected as a domestic candidate for World Heritage on September 3, 2026, and a seminar to advance its inscription will be held in Hikone City on December 5. See also the related article 'Hikone Castle Chosen as Japan's World Heritage Nomination Candidate: A Step Toward Inscription and Crowdfunding to Repair Its Collapsed Stone Walls.'

On the final day of the conference, November 11, the researchers toured Matsue City. From November 12 to 14, excursions to Izumo, Matsumoto, and Inuyama were also scheduled. When visiting Matsue Castle, please check the keep's opening information in the official guidance of Matsue City. This site's event calendar also lists the 'Hikone Castle World Heritage Registration Seminar' on December 5.
