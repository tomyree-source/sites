Hikone Castle's Pines Get Their Winter 'Komomaki' Straw Wrapping — About 100 Trees, Removed and Burned Next March

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Asahi Shimbun (November 8, 2026). On the morning of Ritto, the start of winter, on November 7, Hikone Castle in Hikone City, Shiga Prefecture, got its winter preparations. Staff of the Hikone Castle Operation and Management Center wrapped straw komo around about 100 pines in total — the 'Iroha-matsu' avenue of 32 trees along the inner moat, the Genkyu-en Garden, a daimyo garden, and the plaza in front of the keep.

Komomaki is a winter tradition in which straw mats are wrapped around pine trunks to protect the trees from pests. The komo are scheduled to be removed and burned on Keichitsu, March 6 next year, the day insects are said to awaken. According to Chunichi Shimbun (November 7, 2026), 14 staff members of the Hikone Castle Operation and Management Center wrapped the trees efficiently that morning.

In September, Hikone Castle was chosen as Japan's domestic recommendation candidate for World Heritage listing. The center's director said autumn visitors feel about 30 percent more numerous than last season, and added, 'You can enjoy the scenery in every season, so please come in winter too.' A seminar on Hikone Castle's World Heritage registration will be held on December 5 at Proceed Arena HIKONE (also listed in this site's event calendar).

For exam study, the key points are Hikone Castle and this winter care tradition. Hikone Castle is a hill castle begun in 1604 by Ii Naotsugu and Naotaka and completed in 1622, and its keep is a National Treasure. Genkyu-en Garden is a daimyo garden, and together with the plaza in front of the keep it is one of the castle's highlights. Komomaki, wrapping pines against pests, is a winter maintenance custom carried out at castles and gardens across Japan, with the straw removed and burned at Keichitsu in spring.
