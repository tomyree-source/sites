Kitano Tenmangu Shrine in Kyoto: Nighttime Maple Illumination at the Historic Odoi Momiji-en Garden from November 13

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Leaf KYOTO. At Kitano Tenmangu Shrine in Kamigyo Ward, Kyoto City, Kyoto Prefecture, a special opening and illumination of the “Historic Odoi Momiji-en” maple garden are being held. The special opening runs from Saturday, October 31 to Sunday, December 6, 2026, and the illumination from Friday, November 13 to Sunday, December 6.

The Momiji-en is a celebrated spot with about 350 maple trees, making use of part of the surviving “Odoi,” the earthen rampart built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in the Momoyama period. Making the most of the Odoi's structure, the garden offers both a view looking down on the maples from above and a view looking up at them from below. The surface of the Kamiya River, which flows from north to south, the stage set up at the teahouse, and the observation points are also introduced as highlights. During the illumination, you can enjoy a fantastical world of autumn leaves spread out all around.

The special opening runs from October 31 to December 6, 2026, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. (last admission 3:40 p.m.). The illumination runs from November 13 to December 6, from sunset to 8:00 p.m. (last admission 7:40 p.m.). Admission is 1,500 yen for adults and 700 yen for children, with tea and sweets included; preschool children are free. Please note that the details may change, so check the latest information in Kitano Tenmangu's official announcements before you go.

This connects with themes in shrine exam study. First, “Kitano Tenmangu and Sugawara no Michizane.” Kitano Tenmangu is a Tenmangu shrine enshrining Sugawara no Michizane, and is also known as a famous spot for plum blossoms. Second, the “Odoi.” Part of this earthen rampart, built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi to encircle the city of Kyoto, still remains today. Third, “historic sites and special openings.” A garden that makes use of part of a historic site is opened for a limited period, letting you encounter scenery you cannot usually see. The Momiji-en is at once a famous spot for autumn leaves and a historical structure — an earthen rampart. That double way of seeing it is what makes the Momiji-en so interesting.

The Momiji-en has two faces: autumn leaves by day and illumination by night. When you visit Kitano Tenmangu, check the opening hours and last admission times in the official announcements before setting out.

A word from Suzu: Looking down from on top of the earthen rampart, looking up from below, and at night wrapped in light — it's amazing how much the same autumn leaves change depending on where and when you view them. I'm looking forward to the evening sessions starting November 13, too.
