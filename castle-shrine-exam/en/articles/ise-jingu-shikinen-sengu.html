Ise Jingu and the Shikinen Sengu: The Once-in-20-Years Rebuilding Ritual with 1,300 Years of History

Ise Jingu, in the city of Ise in Mie Prefecture, holds a special place among shrines. Its formal name is simply 'Jingu,' and it consists of the Naiku and the Geku at its center, together with many associated shrines, large and small. The great ritual that has continued at Ise Jingu for nearly 1,300 years is the Shikinen Sengu.

【Naiku and Geku】The deity of the Naiku (Kotai Jingu) is Amaterasu Omikami, regarded as the ancestral deity of the Imperial Family. The Geku (Toyouke Daijingu) enshrines Toyouke Omikami, regarded as a deity of industry, including food, clothing and shelter. Ise Jingu is often introduced with the visiting order 'from the Geku to the Naiku.' On the Shrine Certification exam, too, the difference between the deities of the Naiku and the Geku is asked as basic knowledge.

【How the Shikinen Sengu Works】The Shikinen Sengu is a ritual held once every 20 years, in which the shrine buildings, beginning with the main sanctuary, are rebuilt and the sacred object of worship is transferred to the new shrine. Next to the sanctuary there is always a plot of the same size kept ready (the mishikichi, or sacred site), and the new buildings are raised there. The sacred garments and treasures are also newly made for the occasion. That the buildings are always kept fresh and new is sometimes explained through the idea of 'tokowaka' — everlasting youth.

【A Cycle of 1,300 Years】The first Shikinen Sengu is said to have been held in the reign of Empress Jito (690), and the most recent, the 62nd, took place in 2013. The next is scheduled for 2033. The 20-year cycle is also pointed out to have served as a milestone at which master shrine carpenters and craft artisans hand their techniques down to the younger generation.

【Sanctuaries in the Shinmei-zukuri Style】The main sanctuaries of the Naiku and the Geku are built in the shinmei-zukuri style. Raised floors on posts dug into the ground, gabled roofs, and the forked chigi and log-like katsuo-gi ornaments form a plain yet powerful style — one of the oldest styles, also taken up in our article on shrine architecture. The same form has been handed down at every rebuilding.

【Exam Study Points】This is a top-priority theme spanning Chapters 4 and 5 of the Shrine Certification textbook. Be sure to fix three points: 'the Shikinen Sengu is held once every 20 years,' 'the Naiku enshrines Amaterasu Omikami and the Geku enshrines Toyouke Omikami,' and 'the main sanctuaries are in the shinmei-zukuri style.'

If you have the chance to visit Ise Jingu, turn your eyes also to the mishikichi, the plot spreading beside the sanctuary. It is the place where preparations for the next Sengu are quietly under way.
