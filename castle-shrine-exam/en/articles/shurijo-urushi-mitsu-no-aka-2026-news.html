The Red of the 'Red Castle' Has Three Faces — Shuri Castle's Lacquer Holds the Wisdom of Its Restoration

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Okinawa News QAB (November 10, 2026, carried by Yahoo! News). Four years after restoration work began, Shuri Castle's main hall opens to the public on November 23. In a feature on the restored hall's highlights, QAB turned to the first thing that catches the eye — the vivid red of its lacquerwork.

Shuri Castle, sometimes called a giant piece of Ryukyu lacquerware, is beloved at home and abroad as the 'red castle.' That symbolic red is in fact expressed with three different pigments. The first is toshu, used on the kohai-bashira — the columns of the front porch of the main hall. It is the most vivid and lustrous of the reds, drawing the eye and conveying solemn dignity and sacredness within its beauty.

The second is Kume red clay (Kume-akatsuchi), a high-quality clayey red earth from Kume Island and a natural pigment also used in traditional Kumejima tsumugi textiles. Following an old document's note that Kume red clay was mixed into lacquer and applied, the restoration used it on the second-floor handrails and lattice windows. The third is Kushimagiri bengara, revived after some 200 years. Its raw material is the red water called 'kaimiji' in northern Okinawa Island, natural iron oxide produced by bacteria living in the spring water, and old documents record it as an offering to the royal government. In this restoration it was applied to most of the main structure, including the outer walls and pillars, becoming the 'main vermilion' that wraps the whole building.

Toshu, Kume red clay, and Kushimagiri bengara — three kinds of red combine into a beautifully harmonious whole. Old documents and natural materials were the clues that brought a lost color back to life.

For exam study, the key point is the thinking behind the 'Reiwa restoration' of Shuri Castle's main hall. Shuri Castle was the castle of the Ryukyu Kingdom from the 15th to the 19th centuries; its main hall burned down in the fire of October 31, 2019, and restoration has been under way since 2022. A hallmark of the work is 'visible reconstruction' — carrying out the restoration in public view. The lacquerwork shows how far the research reaches, down to choosing pigments again from the records in old documents. See also the related article 'How Is This Restoration Different? — Shuri Castle's Main Hall and the Reiwa Restoration Ahead of Its November 23 Public Opening.'

Shuri Castle's main hall opens to the public from noon on Monday, November 23. Entry inside the main hall is by advance reservation for a designated time slot. Before visiting, please check the latest information in Shuri Castle Park's official guidance. This site's event calendar also lists the Ryutan Pond illumination (until February 27, 2027), with a view of Shuri Castle.

A word from Tsurugi: One red holds three pigments and a 200-year story. Once it opens, I hope you will compare the toshu on the porch columns with the bengara on the outer walls.
