Hikone Castle Chosen as Japan's World Heritage Nomination Candidate: A Step Toward Inscription and Crowdfunding to Repair Its Collapsed Stone Walls

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Sources: the Asahi Shimbun (November 5, 2026) and READYFOR, from the Cultural Properties Section of the Hikone City Tourism, Culture and Sports Department, Shiga Prefecture (published September 1, 2026). A seminar to promote Hikone Castle's inscription on the World Heritage List will be held on December 5, 2026, in Hikone City, Shiga Prefecture. On September 3 this year, Hikone Castle was selected as Japan's domestic candidate for World Heritage nomination — a milestone reached 34 years after it was placed on Japan's Tentative List. In addition, a second round of crowdfunding to restore the collapsed “Komekura Suimon Ishigaki” (the stone wall of the Rice Storehouse Water Gate) continues its challenge until November 30.

Thirty-four years after being placed on Japan's Tentative List, Hikone Castle became the domestic candidate for World Heritage nomination on September 3, 2026. The seminar will be held on December 5 from 1:00 p.m. at Proceed Arena HIKONE in Koizumi-cho, Hikone City, Shiga Prefecture. Building on the precedent of the “Asuka-Fujiwara Palace Capitals” (Asuka Village, Kashihara City and other locations in Nara Prefecture), inscribed on the World Heritage List in July 2026, the seminar will consider how the value of Hikone Castle should be understood within the local community. Professor Daisuke Furuya of Osaka University's Graduate School will give a keynote lecture on the theme “World Heritage and Hikone Castle,” and Katsue Koike of the Asuka Village Board of Education will report on the significance of the Asuka-Fujiwara inscription and on the preservation and passing down of the sites. The event is hosted by the Hikone Castle World Heritage Inscription Promotion Council, formed by the prefecture and the city; admission is free and limited to the first 200 applicants. Please apply by December 3 through the event management site “Peatix.”

Hikone Castle's “Komekura Suimon Ishigaki” collapsed in July 2024 during three days of torrential rain. It is the stone wall of a gate attached to a complex of 17 rice storehouses, built after the Siege of Osaka to store 50,000 bales of castle rice entrusted by the shogunate. Then, at the end of June 2026, heavy rain from Typhoon No. 7 and other storms caused the wall on the opposite side to collapse as well. The second crowdfunding campaign, “The Miraculous Castle — Together for the Future of National Treasure Hikone Castle: Round 2, Restoring the Komekura Suimon Ishigaki,” began on September 1, 2026; it reached its first goal of 10 million yen and is now challenging its second goal of 20 million yen. It ends at 11:00 p.m. on Monday, November 30. This fiscal year, about 34 million yen in project costs will be needed to advance the survey and design work for dismantling the collapsed stone walls this year and beginning to re-stack them next fiscal year, and the re-stacking itself is expected to require a further 53 million yen or so.

At Hikone Castle, not only is the keep a National Treasure; the entire castle site is designated a Special Historic Site. The grounds cover about 489,932 square meters — roughly the area of ten Tokyo Domes. The stone walls are said to extend for more than 15 km in total. Hikone Castle's stone walls are also testimony that conveys the castle's own history to the present day.

Hikone Castle is one of the twelve surviving original keeps, and its keep is a National Treasure. That the entire castle site is designated a Special Historic Site is also a point often asked about in the exams. It became the domestic candidate for World Heritage nomination on September 3, 2026. The collapse and restoration of the stone walls is a story that shows the present-day front lines of work to protect cultural properties.
