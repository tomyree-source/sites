Shuri Castle's Seiden Main Hall to Reopen to the Public on November 23 After the 2019 Fire

This is a news report on the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. The source is the Mainichi Shimbun (October 25, 2026). At Shuri Castle in Naha, the reconstruction of the Seiden main hall, begun in 2022, is nearing completion, and the hall will open to the general public on November 23. The main hall lost in the 2019 fire is about to appear before people once again.

The 2019 fire, and a castle rebuilt again and again — On October 31, 2019, a fire broke out at Shuri Castle, and seven buildings including the Seiden were completely destroyed. Yet this was not the first time in history that Shuri Castle's buildings had been lost to fire. In the Ryukyu Kingdom era there were three fires, and each time the castle was rebuilt. In the Second World War, too, it was devastated by American attacks in 1945. After the war, when the University of the Ryukyus, which had been built on the site, relocated, calls for restoration grew, and the Seiden was completed in 1992. The current reconstruction and reopening are the latest page in this long history of being lost and built anew.

The political and diplomatic center of the Ryukyu Kingdom — For roughly 450 years, Shuri Castle was home to the kings of the Ryukyu Kingdom and the center of its politics and religious ceremonies. The kingdom was linked to Ming and Qing China through tributary trade, and Shuri Castle also served as a diplomatic base, hosting envoys. In 1879, with the 'Ryukyu Disposition,' the Ryukyu Kingdom was annexed into Japan to become Okinawa Prefecture, and the king was made to leave Shuri Castle. Among Japanese castles, Shuri's great distinction is that it was at once a royal palace and a stage for diplomacy.

New findings and a 'reconstruction on display' — While following the basic design, this reconstruction is distinguished by also incorporating new knowledge gained from later research. The paint on the outer walls and elsewhere was found to be a natural pigment known as Kushi-magiri bengara, giving a deeper red than in the previous restoration. Another distinctive feature: to raise interest among prefectural residents and tourists, a viewing area was set up at the construction site under the theme of a 'reconstruction on display.' According to Okinawa Prefecture, more than 6 billion yen in donations has been collected so far, of which about 4.3 billion yen has been applied to part of the reconstruction costs. The remainder, it is said, will be used for the reconstruction of buildings such as the Hokuden (North Hall) and Nanden (South Hall) that begins from here, and for training people to carry on traditional building techniques.

Exam study points — Themes that connect with Castle Exam study. First, the Ryukyu Kingdom and Shuri Castle's role: Shuri was the king's residence and at the same time the center of politics, religious ritual and diplomacy. Second, the reflection of new findings in reconstruction: reconstruction is not mere replication — it is also an endeavor that raises accuracy by incorporating what advancing research has revealed. Third, the meaning of a 'reconstruction on display': by opening the construction process itself to the public, interest is kept alive and spread into donations and the training of successors.

The Seiden opens to the general public from November 23. If you have the chance to visit Shuri Castle, gaze at the deep red of its outer walls and let your thoughts turn to the history of this castle, which has burned and been rebuilt again and again.
