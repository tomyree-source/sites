Shuri Castle: Why the Main Hall Plaque Turned Yellow — A Ryukyu-Era Diary Reveals the Truth

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Kyodo News (November 28, 2026, via Tokushima Shimbun Digital). The main hall (Seiden) of Shuri Castle in Naha, Okinawa, destroyed by fire in October 2019 and rebuilt, opened to the public on November 23, 2026. One of the biggest changes inside is the plaque 'Chuzan Seido' hung above the throne on the second floor. In the 1992 Heisei restoration its base was vermilion, but in the Reiwa restoration it turned yellow, and the characters were made with the ukibori technique, carved out separately from the base.

【A Diary Reveals 'Mirror Yellow Lacquer'】 The reason for the change lies in a diary recording how the plaque was made in the Ryukyu Kingdom era. The 'Gyohitsu Gohyougo Narabini Ongaku Oshitatenikki' is a diary written by Iraha Pechin, who made the plaque and its mountings; it is commonly known as 'Shoke Monjo No. 360'. It is held by the Naha City Museum of History, and the Shuri Castle Reconstruction Division of Okinawa Prefecture carried out its restoration. Found anew after the Heisei restoration and gradually deciphered, the diary reads 'kagami kiiro nuri' — mirror yellow lacquer — showing that the plaque's base was yellow and its characters were finished in 'kinpaku migaki' (polished gold leaf). Yuu Suzuki, a curator at the Naha City Museum of History, said, 'Until now we too thought the base was vermilion, so we were surprised — we never imagined yellow could be there until we read the words.'

【'My Proposal Was Wrong'】 Susumu Asato, professor emeritus at Okinawa Prefectural University of Arts, was involved in restoring Shuri Castle and also worked on the plaque before it was lost to fire. He revealed, 'The red colour before the fire was my proposal, but that was a mistake. It was actually yellow.' The man who proposed vermilion in the Heisei restoration accepted the change to yellow in the Reiwa restoration in light of the newly deciphered source. The vermilion of the outer walls also changed, from the vivid red of the Heisei restoration to a deeper vermilion, using natural pigments gathered in northern Okinawa Island. The latest deciphering of old documents has brought the restoration closer to the original appearance.

【For Exam Study】 For exam study, the key point is the plaque and the idea of 'restoration'. A plaque (hengaku) is a framed board hung on a building, and 'Chuzan Seido' is the plaque hung above the throne on the second floor of the main hall. The difference in colour between the Heisei restoration (1992) and the Reiwa restoration comes from new sources being discovered and deciphered, deepening our understanding — showing that a restoration is not something fixed once and for all. See also the related article 'The Plaque Turns Yellow and 33 Dragons — A First Look Inside Shuri Castle's Main Hall.'

The main hall of Shuri Castle has been open to the public since November 23, 2026. To see inside the main hall, a free timed reservation is required together with an admission ticket.
