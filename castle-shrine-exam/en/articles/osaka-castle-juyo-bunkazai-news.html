Osaka Castle's Main Keep Recommended for National Important Cultural Property Status

This is the first of our news reports on the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. The source is announcements by the Agency for Cultural Affairs and the City of Osaka (October 16, 2026). On October 16, 2026, the national Council for Cultural Affairs recommended to the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology that eight structures, including the main keep of Osaka Castle, be newly designated as Important Cultural Properties. The formal designation will take effect once it is published in the Official Gazette.

What this recommendation decides — Designation as an Important Cultural Property proceeds in steps: the Council for Cultural Affairs studies and deliberates, then makes a recommendation to the Minister. This recommendation shows that the process has moved forward for Osaka Castle's main keep. At the recommendation stage, however, it is not yet a formal designation; it becomes an Important Cultural Property only when published in the Official Gazette. When reading news like this, keep the distinction in mind: 'recommendation' means the designation is effectively decided, and 'gazette notice' means it is official.

Today's keep is a reconstruction built with citizens' donations — The present Osaka Castle main keep was built in 1931 with donations from the citizens of Osaka. It is a steel-frame reinforced concrete structure, five tiers on the outside and eight floors inside, and its appearance is said to reproduce the keep of the Toyotomi period, based on research into folding screens and historical documents. Among keeps rebuilt before the war, it is a full-scale reconstruction, positioned as 'the only full-scale reconstructed keep of the prewar period.' The history of Osaka Castle itself — from Toyotomi Hideyoshi's construction through the Tokugawa shogunate's rebuilding to the citizens' reconstruction — is covered in detail in this blog's article on Osaka Castle (see the related link at the end of this article).

From registered property and city designation to national status — Osaka Castle's main keep has steadily built up its standing as a cultural property. In 1997 it became a nationally Registered Tangible Cultural Property, and in July 2025 it was designated a Tangible Cultural Property by the City of Osaka. This recommendation marks the next step: progress toward designation as a national Important Cultural Property. Note that what is being valued is not only the building's age, but the very history of its having been rebuilt by citizens' hands.

Exam study points — This theme ties into Chapters 4 and 5 of the Castle Exam textbook. First, the difference between a reconstructed keep and a surviving original keep. A surviving keep (the 'twelve surviving keeps') is one built in or before the Edo period that remains today; Osaka Castle's keep, rebuilt in 1931 — ahead of the postwar reconstructions — is classed as a 'reconstructed keep.' Second, the difference between a Registered Tangible Cultural Property and an Important Cultural Property. Registration is a system centered on notification, with lighter protection designed to encourage use of the property; designation is a system of fuller, stricter protection, under which changes to the property's existing state require permission. Keep in mind, together with the flow seen here, that the same building can advance from registration to designation.

The formal designation will follow the Official Gazette notice. If further developments arise, we will continue to report them on this blog. When you next visit Osaka Castle, try recalling the story of 'the reconstructed keep that citizens built' as you look up at it. It may change the way you see it.
