Kamado Stoves from Three Houses, Wells in Good Condition — Edo-Period Townhouse Remains Found at the Himeji Castle Town Site

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: the Asahi Shimbun (November 18, 2026). The Himeji City Archaeological Center, which is excavating the castle town site of Himeji Castle in Hyogo Prefecture, announced on November 17 that it has confirmed new Edo-period townhouse remains. A staff member described them as 'valuable remains, with kamado stoves and wells preserved in good condition.' A site briefing will be held on November 21.

【Three Houses' Worth of Townhouses in a Bustling Corner of the Castle Town】The site is on the south side of Shirasagi Elementary and Junior High School. To the north lies the Nakabori moat (now National Route 2), and to the south runs the Saigoku Kaido highway. This was one of the busiest corners of the castle town, with a particularly heavy flow of people. Past excavations nearby have also uncovered merchant houses. In the current excavation area, kamado stoves were found clustered in three groups — three, two, and six stoves from east to west — indicating that at least three houses stood there. Two remains known as 'senretsu buildings,' where roof tiles were set upright around the building foundations, were also found. They are thought to have been storehouses.

【A Record of a Cotton Shop — The Connection with the Finds Is Still to Come】According to the center, there is a record of a shop dealing in cotton in the area, but no artifacts have been found that prove the connection. What kinds of shops lined the streets, and what kind of life was lived there? The clues for reading the castle town will emerge as the excavation and analysis continue.

【For Exam Study】For exam study, the key point is how to read Himeji Castle's castle town and its excavations. Himeji Castle is a National Treasure castle inscribed on the World Heritage list in 1993, but the value of a castle is not only in its keep. The townhouse remains of the castle town reveal the lives of the people who supported the castle. The same Himeji City Archaeological Center is also holding the autumn special exhibition 'TOYOTOMI IMPACT' until December 20, where the first gold-leaf roof tiles discovered in Hyogo Prefecture are on public display for the first time. See also the related article 'Gold-Leaf Roof Tiles, the First Found in Hyogo, on Public Display — Himeji City Archaeological Center TOYOTOMI IMPACT Exhibition, Until December 20.'

When visiting Himeji Castle, please check the viewing information in the official guidance of Himeji City. This site's event calendar also lists the site briefing on November 21 and the Himeji Castle illumination beginning November 20.
