Ueno Tenjin Festival in Iga: Final-Day Danjiri Procession Cut Short by Rain

This is a news report on the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. The source is the Asahi Shimbun (published October 26, 2026), reporting on events of October 25, 2026. The Ueno Tenjin Festival's Danjiri Event, an autumn festival held in the central streets of Iga City, Mie Prefecture, reached its final day. However, the day's main event, the Shinko-sai (the main festival procession), was called off partway through when rain began to fall.

Out at 9 a.m., rained on by 9:30 — At around 9 a.m. on October 25, the mikoshi (portable shrine) procession set out, followed by the oni (demon) procession. But at about 9:30, the rain began. Once the cancellation was decided, the nine danjiri floats were covered with sheets over their roofs and made their way back to their respective storehouses, the danjiri-gura. Behind the splendor of the procession, the report also conveys the on-the-ground judgment calls made to protect the festival's equipment from sudden changes in the weather.

The day the deity was to return to the shrine — The day's main festival was meant to accompany the 'owatari,' the homeward journey of the deity of Ueno Tenjin-gu (Sugawara Shrine), who had been transferred to the Eastern Otabisho in Ueno Kurumazaka-cho, back to the shrine in Ueno Higashi-cho: the oni procession would parade through the streets, and nine danjiri would make their rounds. The deity travels out to the otabisho (a temporary resting place during a festival procession) and, as the festival's finale, returns to the shrine — this round trip forms the backbone of the Shinko-sai. This year, that homeward procession was cut short by the rain.

Cancelled in the tenth-anniversary year of its UNESCO listing — Nakamura, president of the Ueno Cultural Arts Preservation Society, which runs the festival as a whole, said: 'It is a real disappointment that this happened in the tenth year since the Danjiri Event was inscribed as UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage. The children had been practicing the festival music. We want to carry this forward to next year.' The Ueno Tenjin Festival's Danjiri Event is inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list as part of 'Yama, Hoko, Yatai, float festivals in Japan,' a grouping of festival rituals from across the country. The organizers' frustration shows through in this cancellation in a milestone year.

Exam study points — This topic connects with Chapter 4 of the Shrine Exam textbook, 'Festivals and Rituals.' First, the Shinko-sai and the owatari: a Shinko-sai (togyo) is an event in which the spirit of the deity is transferred to a mikoshi and carried around the parish area, and a homeward procession like this one forms part of it. Second, the relationship with the otabisho: the otabisho is where the deity rests partway through the procession; the deity goes out to the otabisho and in due course returns to the shrine. Third, the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage 'Yama, Hoko, Yatai float festivals': note the significance of festival events from many regions — with danjiri, dashi floats and yamaboko — being inscribed together as a single group.

The cancellation was a disappointment, but the decision to protect the danjiri from the rain and return them to their storehouses is itself an important part of carrying the festival on to the next year. We hope that next autumn, festival music and danjiri processions will return once again to the streets of Iga.
