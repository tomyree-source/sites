Shrine Visiting Manners: A Complete Guide to Dress, Photography and Conduct in the Grounds

Our earlier article explained the steps of basic shrine etiquette. This article is its practical companion: a complete summary of the points people tend to hesitate over on site, such as dress and photography. Rather than the fine details of form, it organizes the overall attitude of 'how to behave before the deity.'

How to think about dress — For an ordinary visit there is no fixed dress code. However, as a place for paying respect to the deity, neat and clean attire is the basis. When bowing, it is considered polite to remove hats and sunglasses. For formal worship (seishiki sanpai), such as Shichi-Go-San or weddings, it is customary to attend in formal dress.

Cautions on photography — Photography within the grounds is treated differently by each shrine. Where signs say 'No photography,' following the signs is absolute. Many shrines prohibit photography inside the main and worship halls and during rituals and ceremonies. Also avoid photographs that include other worshippers, and lingering for long in the center of the approach to take pictures. When in doubt, the surest course is to ask a word at the shrine office.

Conduct in the grounds — Considerations such as walking to the side of the approach's center (the seichu), not raising one's voice in a commotion, and eating and drinking only in designated places are expressions of respect for a sacred place. Some shrines place restrictions on bringing pets, and some have rules on the handling of water at the purification basin. The habit of reading the information signs in the grounds is, in the end, the greatest manner of all.

A final check on offerings and bows — Put your offering money in quietly rather than throwing it; do not ring the bell roughly; after two bows, two claps, one bow, settle your heart as you press your palms together. Some shrines also advise that, before making a wish, you first convey your address and name and your gratitude in your heart. More than the correctness of the sequence, performing each gesture with care is the essence of a shrine visit.

Exam study points — A full review of Chapter 3 of the Shrine Exam textbook, 'Walking the Grounds and How to Pray at a Shrine.' Organize around the three points — 'walk avoiding the seichu,' 'at the purification basin, begin with the left hand,' 'two bows, two claps, one bow' — and treat dress and photography under the principle 'follow each shrine's guidance.'

Most manners are, when you boil them down, ways of expressing 'I hold this place dear.' Learn the forms, and then visit with your heart in it — that is the conclusion of this summary.
