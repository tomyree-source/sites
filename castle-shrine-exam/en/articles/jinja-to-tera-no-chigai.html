Shrines vs. Temples in Japan: How to Tell Them Apart by Torii, Gates and Etiquette

From this article, this Shrine Exam blog takes up shrines as its theme. As a starting point for study, let us first organize 'the difference between shrines and temples.' The two often stand side by side, yet their origins and their etiquette differ greatly.

What they enshrine differs — A shrine is a Shinto facility and enshrines kami (deities). The goshintai, the sacred object embodying the deity, is housed in the main hall, and worshippers pray from the worship hall. A temple is a Buddhist facility: its main hall enshrines Buddhist statues, and it is also a place for monks' training and memorial services. The titles of the person in charge differ too: the highest-ranking priest of a shrine is the 'guji,' while the head of a temple is the 'jushoku.'

Tell them apart at the entrance and buildings — The marker of a shrine entrance is the torii; the grounds hold buildings such as the main hall, worship hall and shrine office. As a rule, shrines have no graves. A temple's entrance is the sanmon gate, and besides the main hall many temples have a bell tower, a five-story pagoda and a cemetery. Even if you do not know the place or the building names, whether there is a torii is the easiest clue.

Differences in how to pray — At a shrine, the general etiquette is two bows, two claps, one bow, delivering the sound of clapped hands (kashiwade) to the deity. At a temple, one does not clap; the general practice is to press the palms together before the chest and bow quietly. The presence or absence of an offering box and a bell is another hint for telling them apart.

Once one: the fusion of kami and buddhas — Over a long history, kami and buddhas were believed in as one and the same. This is called shinbutsu-shugo, the fusion of Shinto and Buddhism. It is said to have been institutionally separated by the shinbutsu bunri (separation of kami and buddhas) of the Meiji era, but its traces remain in shrines and temples across Japan — Hachiman, for instance, bears the title of a bodhisattva.

Exam study points — Within the scope of Chapter 1 of the Shrine Exam textbook. The contrast of titles — 'guji at a shrine, jushoku at a temple' — is standard knowledge asked on the exam. Fix it in memory together with the felt sense of how to tell them apart.
