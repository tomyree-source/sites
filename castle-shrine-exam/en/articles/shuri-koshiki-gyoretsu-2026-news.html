Shurijo Castle Reconstruction Festival: The Royal “Koshiki Procession” Returns to the Castle Town on the Final Day

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Kyodo News (November 3, 2026). Around Shurijo Castle in Naha City, Okinawa Prefecture, a “Koshiki Gyoretsu” (traditional ceremonial procession) was held on November 3 as part of the Shurijo Castle Reconstruction Festival, with performers in the roles of the Ryukyu king and queen parading around the castle. It has been seven years since the 2019 fire, and the procession took place with the general opening of the restored Seiden drawing near.

The procession reenacts the “Sankeiji Sankei Gyoko,” the royal visit in which the Ryukyu king toured three temples below Shurijo Castle to pray for peace and abundant harvests. About 220 people, including the performers playing the king and queen in restored Ryukyu costume, paraded solemnly around the area. The surroundings were reportedly enveloped in the atmosphere of the royal dynasty era. A care worker from Naha City (50) was reported as saying, “The burning of Shurijo Castle, which had always been there since I was small, was a shock, but as the rebuilding progresses, my spirits are rising too.”

The Shurijo Castle Reconstruction Festival was held from November 1 to 3, and on the first day the Ryukyu Kingdom Picture Scroll Procession was held on Kokusai-dori. As this blog introduced in its pre-festival article, the Koshiki Procession was scheduled for the final day, and this article is a follow-up report that it was held as planned. The Seiden, which burned down completely, has been restored and is scheduled to open to the public on November 23. With the festival's processions over, the stage now shifts to the opening of the Seiden.

This connects with themes in castle exam study. First, “Koshiki processions and gyoko.” A gyoko is a procession in which a king or noble goes out, and processions that reenact old rituals provide clues for thinking about the relationship between a castle and its castle town. Second, “the Ryukyu Kingdom and Shurijo Castle.” Shurijo Castle was the residence of the kings of the Ryukyu Kingdom and the center of politics, diplomacy and religious ceremonies. Third, “restoration and general opening.” Restoration of the Seiden, which burned down in the 2019 fire, has advanced, and general opening is scheduled to begin on November 23, 2026. Festival processions and the restoration of buildings are both efforts to pass castle culture on to future generations.

The processions are over, and next comes the general opening of the Seiden on November 23. When you visit Shurijo Castle, check the opening arrangements and closed days in Shurijo Castle Park's official announcements before you go.

A word from Tsurugi: The Koshiki Procession we introduced as a scheduled event actually took place, with a procession of about 220 people — it's the moment a plan becomes reality. It's just a little longer until the Seiden opens on November 23.
