Inuyama Castle Heads for a Record 700,000 Visitors — Over 650,000 by the End of October

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: NHK (November 2, 2026). Inuyama Castle, a National Treasure in Inuyama City, Aichi Prefecture, has already passed 650,000 visitors by the end of October — the highest figure ever recorded at this point in the year. The increase is thought to be driven by growing numbers of overseas visitors, and if the pace continues, the castle is expected to top 700,000 visitors in a year for the first time.

The keep of Inuyama Castle is a National Treasure, and it is said to be the oldest of the twelve surviving original keeps in Japan. Its first construction is dated to around 1537 (Tenbun 6), and the wooden keep stands on a bluff overlooking the Kiso River. Together with its castle town, the castle is known for preserving the old form of a Japanese castle.

The castle town is also on the move. At the end of March 2026, Inuyama City designated an area of about 142 hectares around the castle town as a promotion zone for the use of vacant houses under the national vacant-house measures act (Asahi Shimbun, November 8, 2026). A thriving castle and a town that is also a place to live — visitor growth and the preservation and use of the town are advancing together.

For the castle exams, remember three points about Inuyama Castle: it is one of the twelve surviving original keeps and its keep is a National Treasure; it is said to be the oldest keep; and it was first built around 1537 (Tenbun 6) and overlooks the Kiso River. See also the related article “Inuyama Castle and the Kiso River: The River and Castle Town Seen from the National Treasure Keep.”

The visitor figures are from the NHK report of November 2, 2026. With the autumn leaves in season, more people are expected to visit Inuyama Castle together with its castle town. Before you go, please check the latest opening information on the castle's official site.
