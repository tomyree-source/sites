Hikone Castle Tests Yamakago Palanquins and Assist Suits on 140 Stone Steps — A Barrier-Free Experiment

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: TABI LABO (November 18, 2026). At Hikone Castle, a National Treasure in Shiga Prefecture, an experiment was carried out to let people who use wheelchairs, or who feel unsteady walking, climb the steep stone steps toward the castle keep. What was prepared for the trial was the Edo-period means of transport, the yamakago palanquin, together with modern technology.

【The Wall of a 'Special Historic Site' Where No Slope Can Be Built】Hikone Castle, which aims to be inscribed on the World Heritage list in 2028, preserves its National Treasure keep and many other Important Cultural Properties. The paths inside the castle are a continuous run of steep stone steps, a major hurdle for anyone who finds walking on their own difficult. Because the entire castle grounds, including the stone steps, are designated a Special Historic Site, it is extremely difficult to add a permanent slope or handrail afterward, out of consideration for cultural property protection. Adding structures for barrier-free access is restricted, and there was a situation where people had no choice but to give up, thinking that 'if there are stairs, not being able to climb is unavoidable.'

【Yamakago Times Assist Suits — A Route of 140 Steps】To address this challenge, Hikone City planned an experiment to test an environment in which anyone can experience Hikone Castle. The yamakago palanquin borrowed from Takahashi City in Okayama Prefecture was brought in. Wheelchair users rode in the palanquin and were carried to the main bailey by human power. The trial route was about 285 meters round trip, with 140 stone steps and a height difference of about 50 meters, covered in three round trips. Because the physical burden on the bearers was the greatest challenge, items that 'extend the body,' such as the assist suits handled by Techno Associé, were also introduced. According to an announcement in October 2026, participants who wore assist suits and similar items said things like 'it was easier to keep my balance when standing up' and 'the moment I took it off, I realized how much it had been assisting me.'

【For Exam Study】For exam study, the key point is the idea of balancing cultural property protection with barrier-free access. Hikone Castle is a castle whose National Treasure keep survives, and the entire castle grounds are designated a Special Historic Site. This trial, which supports the human side with technology instead of modifying the historic site itself, is a new way of aiming for a castle that everyone can enjoy while protecting its cultural properties. During the experiment, the group did not simply move to the main bailey; a curator also guided them around the castle while explaining its history and scenery. Hikone City says it will continue to consider using this as a sightseeing experience that lets visitors relive Edo-period travel. Challenges remain for full-scale operation, such as securing bearers and improving safety. See also the related article 'Hikone Castle Chosen as a Domestic Candidate for World Heritage — A Step Toward Registration and Crowdfunding to Repair a Collapsed Stone Wall.'

When visiting Hikone Castle, please check the viewing information in the official guidance of Hikone City. This site's event calendar also lists the 'Hikone Castle World Heritage Registration Seminar' on December 5.

A word from Tsurugi: Just because a slope cannot be built, they did not give up. An Edo-period yamakago palanquin and the latest assist suits, climbing 140 stone steps together — teamwork across the ages.
