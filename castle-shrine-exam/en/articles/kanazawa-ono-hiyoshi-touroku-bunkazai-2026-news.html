Wave Carvings of a Port Town Become a National Cultural Property — Seven Kanazawa Buildings Recommended for Registration

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: NHK (November 26, 2026). The Council for Cultural Affairs (chaired by Katsuhiko Hibino) recommended on November 20 that seven buildings at three sites in Kanazawa City, including Ono Hiyoshi Shrine, be registered as National Registered Tangible Cultural Properties by the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology. Once registered, the number of Registered Tangible Cultural Properties in Ishikawa Prefecture will reach 298. Across Japan, 121 buildings were newly recommended, and after official gazette notification the total number of registered properties is expected to reach 15,160 (FNN Prime Online).

【Ono Hiyoshi Shrine: Three Buildings Recommended】 At Ono Hiyoshi Shrine (5-chome, Ono-machi, Kanazawa City), three buildings were recommended: the main hall, the heiden and haiden, and the shinsenjo (sacred food preparation hall). The main hall was built in 1864 (Genji 1) and relocated to its present site in 1894 (Meiji 27). Carvings shaped like raging waves show the distinctive character of a port town. The heiden and haiden date from Meiji 27, and the shinsenjo from 1937 (Showa 12).

【Myokakuji Temple and the Senda Family Residence Also Recommended】 At Myokakuji Temple (Kaneishi-shinmachi, Kanazawa City), two buildings were recommended: the main hall and the sanmon gate with storehouse. The main hall was completed in 1878 (Meiji 11). Its front veranda was enclosed indoors — a distinctive feature thought to protect the interior from strong winds. The sanmon gate, built in the style of a samurai residence's nagayamon gatehouse, was praised as a precious surviving structure. The Senda Family Residence (1-chome, Nagamachi, Kanazawa City) was recommended for its main house and earthen wall. It was the home of the Senda family, former samurai of the Kaga domain, and the main house retains traces of a samurai residence from the domain era. Together with the Edo-period earthen wall, it preserves the historic streetscape of Nagamachi to this day.

【For Exam Study】 For exam study, the key point is the 'Registered Tangible Cultural Property' system. Registered Tangible Cultural Properties are buildings of recognised historical and cultural value entered in a register to promote their preservation and use — a different system from the 'designation' of Important Cultural Properties. The main hall of Ono Hiyoshi Shrine was built in 1864 and relocated in 1894, and it carries the history of Ono, the port town of Kanazawa, to this day. Its wave carvings were recognised for their distinctive port-town character. The recommendation is a step toward registration, which becomes official after gazette notification. When you visit Kanazawa, why not also stop by Ono Hiyoshi Shrine in the port town of Ono, or the samurai residence streets of Nagamachi?
