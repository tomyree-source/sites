Osaka Castle's Keep Turns 95 — Rebuilt with Citizens' Donations, It Survived the War

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: YTV NEWS NNN (November 7, 2026). November 7 is the “Reconstruction Anniversary” of the Osaka Castle keep, which marked 95 years since its reconstruction. A commemorative concert, warrior performances, and a talk show hosted by YTV announcer Morokuni were held in the main bailey. The events were part of the Osaka Castle Autumn Festival 2026 (November 7–8).

The keep burned down twice — in the Summer Siege of Osaka and by lightning — and was rebuilt on November 7, 1931 (Showa 6) with donations from the citizens, surviving the air raids of the Pacific War to reach its 95th year. On October 16, the Council for Cultural Affairs recommended to the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology that the keep be designated a National Important Cultural Property (we introduced this in our October 27, 2026 article).

This year's autumn festival was themed “Toyotomi-ha!” — the Toyotomi faction. It featured an exhibition of places connected with the Toyotomi brothers Hideyoshi and Hidenaga and the Sanada family, a one-day-only “Certificate of Ascent” for visitors to the keep on November 7, a matchlock-gun demonstration by the Osaka Castle Teppo-tai on November 8, and a ceremony marking 20 years of the friendship-castle alliance with Ueda Castle (Sankei News / Osaka Castle Park Management Joint Venture). The keep's special exhibition “Sengoku no Kyodai” runs until November 23, and the special openings of the Tamon Yagura and Sengan Yagura turrets continue on Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays until November 29.

For the castle exams, remember that Osaka Castle was begun by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1583 (Tensho 11) and rebuilt by the Tokugawa shogunate. The present keep is a reconstruction built in 1931 with citizens' donations, and it is a Registered Tangible Cultural Property. In October 2026, the Council for Cultural Affairs recommended its designation as an Important Cultural Property. See also the related article “The History of Osaka Castle and Its Rebuilt Keep: Three Stories of Toyotomi, Tokugawa, and the Citizens.”

The keep has now marked 95 years since its reconstruction. The special exhibition “Sengoku no Kyodai” runs until November 23, and the special openings of the turrets continue on Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays until November 29. Before you go, please check the latest information on the official Osaka Castle Park website.

A word from Tsurugi: Ninety-five years ago, the keep was reborn thanks to the citizens' donations. It survived the war and still towers over the Osaka sky. Happy birthday, Osaka Castle!
