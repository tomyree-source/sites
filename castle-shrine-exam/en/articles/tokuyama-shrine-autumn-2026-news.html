Tokuyama Shrine in Autumn — Red Maples and Golden Ginkgo in Competition at Hiruzen, Maniwa

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: FNN Prime Online (Okayama Broadcasting, November 9, 2026). At Tokuyama Shrine in Hiruzen-Kamitookuyama, Maniwa City, Okayama Prefecture, the autumn leaves of maples and ginkgo trees are at their best. Vivid red maples and golden ginkgo trees color the shrine grounds in autumn.

Tokuyama Shrine's grounds hold two ginkgo trees nearly 20 meters tall, which together with maples spreading red leaves across their branches create a beautiful scene. According to local people, the maples began coloring in part in late September, while the ginkgo trees turned color all at once over the past week. A visitor said, 'You rarely get to see the contrast of yellow and red like this — it makes me happy to see it.' Hiruzen is a highland in northern Okayama Prefecture, known as a famous spot for autumn leaves.

At night, a light-up is held, and visitors can enjoy the autumn leaves glowing in the light. The autumn leaves at Tokuyama Shrine can be enjoyed for the rest of this week, weather permitting. The best time for autumn leaves varies from year to year, so please check the latest information before you go.

The autumn leaves of a shrine are one of the pleasures of visiting in autumn. At shrines, the trees in the grounds have long been carefully protected. When you visit, walk quietly through the grounds, and when taking photographs, take care not to disturb other visitors. Ginkgo is one of the trees often planted at shrines, and in autumn it dyes the grounds golden. The autumn leaves at Tokuyama Shrine are at their best for the rest of this week. Before heading to Hiruzen, please check the latest autumn-leaf information.

A word from Suzu: The red of the maples and the gold of the ginkgo — in an autumn shrine, the layering of colors is the greatest feast of all. Don't miss this week's best viewing.
