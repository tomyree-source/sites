The Giant Snow Sculpture Will Be Iwaki-san Shrine's Romon Gate — Hirosaki Castle Snow Lantern Festival Honors a Gate Under Repair

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: Web To-o Nippo (November 14, 2026). The organizing committee of the 51st Hirosaki Castle Snow Lantern Festival, to be held February 10–14, 2027 in Hirosaki Park, Hirosaki City, Aomori Prefecture, met on November 13 and decided that the festival's symbol — the giant snow sculpture — will take the form of the Romon gate of Iwaki-san Shrine.

The Romon gate of Iwaki-san Shrine was built in 1628 (Kan'ei 5). The shrine is currently carrying out large-scale repairs made necessary by aging and snow damage, and since the end of the Oyama-sankei pilgrimage season in September 2026 it has been pressing ahead with repairs to the Romon gate as well as the Chumon gate and the Haiden hall. Work on the Haiden began in fiscal 2025, and in fiscal 2026 the project will build an access road for transporting repair materials and erect a large temporary roof so that work can continue through the winter. The whole project aims for completion by fiscal 2030.

Hirosaki City is supporting these repairs through a community-wide effort. Under the project 'Repairing Hirosaki's Important Cultural Property Buildings,' the city is raising donations through government crowdfunding on the Furusato Choice portal. The target is 18,364,000 yen, and donations are accepted until December 28, 2026. The funds will subsidize repair and preservation work on five buildings of the nationally designated Important Cultural Property Iwaki-san Shrine — the Haiden, Romon, Okumon, Mizugaki, and Chumon — and on the Byosho-mon gate of the nationally designated Important Cultural Property Kosho Shrine. The Byosho-mon gate collapsed under the heavy snow at the start of 2025, and disaster-recovery work is scheduled for fiscal 2026. Hirosaki City has 23 nationally designated Important Cultural Property buildings, comprising 45 structures and three bases — the sixth-largest number among Japanese municipalities.

For exam study, the key point is Hirosaki Castle, Iwaki-san Shrine, and the preservation of cultural properties in snowy regions. Hirosaki Castle is the center of the Tsugaru clan's castle town and is also known as a castle whose keep is being moved back into place. Iwaki-san Shrine is an ancient shrine rooted in mountain worship, and its surviving shrine buildings still convey the atmosphere of the age when Shinto and Buddhism were intertwined. In snowy regions, preparing for snow damage and passing on traditional techniques are major challenges in preserving shrines and castles.
