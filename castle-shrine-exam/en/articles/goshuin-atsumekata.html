How to Collect Goshuin (Shrine and Temple Stamps): The Basics and Etiquette for Receiving Proof of Your Visit

Collecting goshuin (shrine and temple stamps) has taken root as a pleasure of visiting shrines and temples. People opening their stamp books at travel destinations are now an everyday sight at shrines and temples all over Japan. Before you start collecting, get the origins and the etiquette straight.

【The Origins of Goshuin】The origin of the goshuin is said to have been the reception stamp (nokei-in) given when you dedicated a hand-copied sutra to a temple. From there it gradually spread into something received from shrines and temples as proof of a visit. A goshuin, with the name of the shrine or the deity written in ink and stamps pressed over it, is treated as something different in nature from a casual souvenir stamp.

【The Basics of the Stamp Book】Goshuin are received in a dedicated stamp book (goshuincho). Accordion-fold books are the standard, and some people receive stamps on both sides of the pages. Some people keep separate books for shrines and for temples, while others collect in a single book. Some shrines and temples will only write in their own stamp books, or offer only pre-written sheets (kakioki), so it is reassuring to check at the award office (juyosho).

【Etiquette When Receiving One】The basic order is: first complete your worship, then ask at the shrine office or award office. Reception hours are set by each shrine and temple, and during festivals and the like they may not be able to respond. Hand over your stamp book politely with both hands, and when you receive it, rather than opening and admiring it on the spot, express your thanks and take it away. Checking the usual amount of the offering in advance — the hatsuho-ryo at shrines, the nokei-ryo at temples — will keep you calm on the day.

【Enjoy with Moderation】Limited goshuin, changing monthly or tied to festivals, are increasing, but a goshuin is at heart a record of worship. Collecting them for resale is discouraged by many shrines and temples. Keep your book carefully as a record of the places and dates you visited, and later turn its pages to remember the journey — that is the original way to enjoy goshuin.

【Exam Study Points】Goshuin themselves are not directly within the textbook's scope, but the order — 'receive one after you have worshipped' — is deeply connected with how to walk the precincts and the flow of worship learned in Chapter 3 of the Shrine Certification textbook. Understand them as a set with the etiquette of purifying your hands and praying.

It may look similar to the stamp rallies familiar from this site's castle touring, but a goshuin is proof of worship based in faith. Collect with that difference in mind, and the weight of your book will change.
