Nijo Castle Festival 2026 in Kyoto: The World Heritage Castle Dyed in Autumn Colors, Illumination Until December 5

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Source: a press release by Kyoto City (October 19, 2026). The “Nijo Castle Festival 2026” is being held at the World Heritage site, the former imperial villa Nijo Castle (Nakagyo Ward, Kyoto City), for 45 days from Friday, October 23 to Sunday, December 6, 2026. The nighttime illumination event “NAKED meets Nijo Castle 2026” is held over 44 days, from Friday, October 23 to Saturday, December 5, from 6:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m. (closing at 10:00 p.m.). Daytime and nighttime are on a replacement system, and each requires a ticket.

The viewing route and areas for the illumination differ from those in the daytime. The regular price of the nighttime ticket is 2,000 yen for high school students and above from Monday to Thursday, and 2,400 yen on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays; elementary and junior high school students and preschool children are free. However, visitors of junior high school age and below cannot come on their own and must be accompanied by a guardian. Regular daytime viewing is from 8:45 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. (last entry 4:00 p.m.; on December 6, last entry is noon), and admission is 1,300 yen for general visitors, 400 yen for junior and senior high school students, and 300 yen for elementary school students (including the Ninomaru Goten viewing fee).

During the period, many events associated with the Kanei Imperial Visit and Nijo Castle will be held, including an exhibition of Kyoto specialty products, a maple stamp rally, refined cultural experiences such as ikebana (tachibana flower arrangement) and waka poetry, an exhibition and guided tours exploring Kamo shichiseki stones and the castle's stones, and a special demonstration of equestrian archery etiquette. On Monday, November 23 (holiday), the “KYOTO Miyabi STAGE — Kyoto Japanese Culture Festa” will also be held, using the Koun-tei as its venue, with the Imperial Visit Scroll Mini Parade (from 11:30 a.m.), the “Beauty of Kanei” Japanese-dress runway (from 1:30 p.m.), and the “Resonance of Elegance” stage production (from 2:30 p.m.), all free to view (the same event was also held on October 25).

This connects with themes in castle exam study. First, “Nijo Castle is a castle without a keep.” The center of Nijo Castle is the Ninomaru Goten palace, a National Treasure, and it is important not to confuse it with the organization of the twelve surviving original keeps. Second, “its character as the shogun's lodgings.” Tokugawa Ieyasu built it in 1603; it became the base of Tokugawa rule over Kyoto, serving as the shogun's lodgings when he came up to the capital, and in 1867 the restoration of imperial rule (Taisei Hokan) was proclaimed at the Ninomaru Goten. Third, “its position as a World Heritage site.” Nijo Castle is one of the “Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto” World Heritage sites; keep it in mind as a place that, while a castle, conveys palace architecture.

Because daytime and nighttime operate on a replacement system, check the tickets and times for day and night respectively before you visit. For details of the events, please check Kyoto City's announcements and Nijo Castle's official information.

A word from Tsurugi: Even without a keep, the palace, gardens and stone walls speak of the shogun's authority — that's what makes Nijo Castle so interesting. A trip comparing the castle's different faces — the refined events by day and the illumination by night — sounds enjoyable, too.
