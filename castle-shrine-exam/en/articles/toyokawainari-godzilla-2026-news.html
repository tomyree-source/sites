Toyokawa Inari Meets Godzilla: A 1954 and 2026 Connection in the “Godzilla -0.0” Collaboration

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Sources: a press release from the religious corporation Myōgonji Temple (November 4, 2026, published on PR TIMES). At Toyokawa Inari (Enpukuzan Myōgonji Temple) in Toyokawa City, Aichi Prefecture, a grand unveiling (gokaichō) is being held from Sunday, November 1 to Monday, November 23, 2026 — the first in 72 years, since 1954. To mark the occasion, a collaboration with the movie “Godzilla -0.0,” released on Tuesday, November 3, has begun. Toyokawa Inari is affectionately known as an “Inari,” but formally it is Myōgonji Temple, a Sōtō Zen Buddhist temple.

Two events that began in the same year, 1954. The first “Godzilla” movie was released on November 3, 1954, and in that same year Toyokawa Inari held its previous grand unveiling. Then, on November 3, 2026, the latest film, “Godzilla -0.0,” was released — once again coinciding with a grand unveiling. The press release puts it this way: “A grand unveiling is an act of carrying the prayers and the bonds between people that have been passed down into the next age. That work has something in common with the movie ‘Godzilla.’” “Godzilla -0.0” is the 31st film in the Godzilla series (as a domestically produced live-action work). As with the previous film, “Godzilla -1.0,” direction, screenplay, and visual effects are by Takashi Yamazaki, and the cast includes Ryunosuke Kamiki and Minami Hamabe, among others.

Two sumi-e paintings, drawn specially for the collaboration. As the first phase of the collaboration, sumi-e artist Yuki Nishimoto has drawn two special new works depicting Godzilla as he appears in “Godzilla” (1954) and in “Godzilla -0.0.” The exhibition runs from Tuesday, November 3 to Sunday, December 6, 2026, at “Za,” a space inside the Toyokawa Inari Yuki Nishimoto Museum. Limited-edition collaboration goods featuring the newly drawn sumi-e works are also on sale, and a second phase with new developments is planned from mid-December. Nishimoto, born in 1988 and from Kagoshima Prefecture, is a sumi-e artist who has also created Godzilla-themed works for the “Godzilla Record Project” (2020–2021). At Toyokawa Inari, he is working on the large fusuma-e (sliding-door painting) project “Toyokawa Shin Chōjū Giga” as part of the grand unveiling commemorative program.

For the shrine exams, three themes connect with this story. First, “What is a grand unveiling (gokaichō)?” A grand unveiling is an event in which a normally hidden sacred image is specially shown to the public for a limited period. At Toyokawa Inari, the front-standing statue of Toyokawa Dakini Shinten, the temple's guardian deity, is being specially shown. Second, “the spread of Inari faith.” The Inari deity has been widely worshipped at shrines and temples across Japan as a guardian of food, business, and daily life. Third, “the milestone of 72 years.” Both the previous unveiling and this one fall in the Year of the Horse, and the 72-year interval itself tells the story of how precious this event is. See also the related article “Toyokawa Inari in Aichi: The First Gokaicho in 72 Years Begins — Special Opening of the Secret Image from November 1 to 23.”

The grand unveiling runs until Monday, November 23 (a national holiday), and the Godzilla sumi-e exhibition runs until Sunday, December 6. The Grand Unveiling Festival on November 21 and 22 is expected to be crowded. Before you go, please check the latest information on the official grand unveiling website.

A word from Suzu: A bond that overlaps after 72 years, from 1954 to 2026. Sumi-e Godzilla and a hidden Buddha's grand unveiling — an unexpected combination, but both are stories about carrying forward what has been passed down. The exhibition runs until December 6, so you can still enjoy it after the unveiling ends.
