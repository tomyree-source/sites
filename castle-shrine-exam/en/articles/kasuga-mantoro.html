Kasuga Taisha Guide: The Mantoro Lantern Festival and the Fujiwara Clan Shrine in Nara

Kasuga Taisha in Nara City is said to have been founded in the Nara period as the shrine of the Fujiwara clan's tutelary deities. It is known for its vermilion corridors and the stone lanterns that fill its grounds, and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage site 'Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara.'

The Fujiwara tutelary shrine and its four deities — The enshrined deities of Kasuga Taisha are Takemikazuchi-no-mikoto, Futsunushi-no-mikoto, Ame-no-koyane-no-mikoto and Himegami — four in all, together called the Four Deities of Kasuga. As the Fujiwara rose to prosperity, devotion to the shrine grew with them, and the tradition of rebuilding the shrine roughly every twenty years (shikinen zotai) has also been handed down.

The main halls are Kasuga-zukuri — The main sanctuary of Kasuga Taisha consists of four buildings designated National Treasures, in the style called Kasuga-zukuri. As noted in our article on shrine architecture, it is a style that, alongside nagare-zukuri, influenced shrines across Japan, characterized by the gabled roof extending out long over the front. It is also within the scope of Chapter 2 of the Shrine Exam textbook.

The night of the Mantoro — Kasuga Taisha is said to have about 3,000 donated lanterns in all, stone lanterns and hanging lanterns together. The event at which every one of them is lit is the 'Mantoro' (Festival of Ten Thousand Lanterns), held twice a year: on the night of Setsubun and at the Chugen Mantoro in mid-August. The rows of hanging lanterns along the main shrine's corridors and the lines of stone lanterns along the approach embody a devotion to light that has continued for over a thousand years.

Wisteria and the deer of the shrine — In connection with the Fujiwara clan, wisteria is the symbol of Kasuga Taisha. Wisteria in the grounds, such as the 'sunazuri no fuji,' forms a seasonal highlight. Deer, regarded as messengers of the deity, are carefully protected across the Nara Park area; their origin is told in the legend that Takemikazuchi arrived at this land riding a white deer.

Exam study points — A theme spanning Chapters 2 and 5 of the Shrine Exam textbook. Start with the three basics — 'Kasuga Taisha = tutelary shrine of the Fujiwara,' 'its main halls are Kasuga-zukuri,' 'the Mantoro is held twice a year' — and also remember the legend of the deer as the deity's messenger.

Visit on a Mantoro night, and the sheer number of lights makes you feel the accumulation of prayers over long ages. Alongside a daytime visit, do try to attend a nighttime event if you have the chance.
