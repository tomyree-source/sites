A Day of Thanks for the Harvest: Niinamesai Observed at Shrines Across Japan on November 23

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Sources: Miyatele News NNN and MRT Miyazaki Broadcasting (both November 23, 2026). November 23 is Labor Thanksgiving Day, but it began as Niinamesai, the day of giving thanks to the gods for the year's harvest. Today, Niinamesai was observed at the Imperial Palace and at shrines across Japan.

【'Takekoma no Mai' Offered at Takekoma Shrine】At Takekoma Shrine in Iwanuma City, Miyagi Prefecture, about 60 people attended the Niinamesai rite. Newly harvested Hitomebore rice from this autumn, along with vegetables and fruit from across the prefecture, were offered before the gods. This season, poor sunshine and long rains raised concerns about the rice crop, but at Takekoma Shrine the harvest was as plentiful as in a normal year. Afterwards, four shrine maidens in vivid red costumes offered 'Takekoma no Mai,' a sacred dance performed holding ears of ripened rice, and those attending gave thanks for the year's bounty.

【Also at Miyazaki Jingu and Shrines Across Japan】Niinamesai was also held at Miyazaki Jingu in Miyazaki City, attended by more than 100 people from the local JA agricultural cooperative and businesses. Rice and vegetables harvested this year were offered before the gods, a norito prayer was recited, and a dance by shrine maidens was offered. At Takaoka Sekino Shrine in Takaoka City, Toyama Prefecture, Niinamesai was observed from 10:00 a.m., and the new rice offered before the gods was shared with attendees as osagari rice balls. On the same day, the shrine grounds also hosted the 'Taka no Miya Market,' with about 20 stalls offering fortune-telling, handmade goods and more (Takaoka Sekino Shrine). At Kashihara Jingu in Nara Prefecture, Niinamesai is observed every year at 11:00 a.m. on November 23, with the Kume Mai dance offered. The 54th Kashihara City Agricultural Festival is also held at the same time in the square in front of the outer worship hall (Kashihara City).

【For Exam Study】For exam study, the key point is what Niinamesai means. Niinamesai is one of the imperial court rituals, a harvest festival. 'Ni' means the new grain harvested this year, and 'name' means to taste it: the new rice of the year is first offered to the gods, thanks are given for the harvest, and gods and people share the offering together — the spirit of 'shinjin kyoshoku,' eating together with the gods. Before the war, November 23 was the national holiday 'Niinamesai,' but with the National Holidays Act of 1948 it became Labor Thanksgiving Day. See also the related article 'A 1,300-Year Cycle of Rebuilding Every 20 Years — Ise Jingu Begins Cutting Timber for Its 63rd Shikinen Sengu.' At Ise Jingu today, the '6th Shin-on Kansha Ise Shinshu Festival' is also being held to celebrate Niinamesai.

Niinamesai is a festival of giving thanks for the year's harvest and praying for abundance in the year to come. Next year too, on November 23, shrines across Japan will observe Niinamesai. Our events calendar also lists today's Niinamesai and the Ise Shinshu Festival.

A word from Suzu: Offering the new rice and sharing it together — a festival where the gods and people sit at the same table. How warm. Today's 'itadakimasu' may feel a little more special than usual.
