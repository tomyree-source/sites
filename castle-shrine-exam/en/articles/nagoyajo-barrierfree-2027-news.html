Nagoya Castle's Barrier-Free Plan Pushed to Next Fiscal Year — Wooden Keep Reconstruction Costs Set to Top 50.5 Billion Yen

This is a news article bringing you the latest developments concerning castles and shrines. Sources: FNN Prime Online (Tokai TV, November 4, 2026) and Chunichi Shimbun Web (November 4, 2026). Nagoya City announced on November 4 that it will postpone finalizing the barrier-free plan for the wooden reconstruction of Nagoya Castle's keep to fiscal 2027. The city plans to install a 'vertical lift' in the reconstructed wooden keep; while it is physically possible to install one up to the top floor, discussions continue over how many floors it is appropriate to serve from a cultural perspective.

Nagoya City had been in dialogue with organizations of people with disabilities and aimed to finalize the plan by February next year. However, with the matters under discussion growing, the finalization will now be postponed to next fiscal year. On November 4, a meeting attended by disability organizations was held at the Nagoya Urban Center in Naka Ward, Nagoya, where the city explained the postponement. Takahiko Watanabe, director of the Nagoya Castle General Office, said, 'We are carefully considering how to balance the wooden reconstruction with barrier-free access. We are sorry it is taking time.' At meetings from February next year onward, the city will present a draft barrier-free plan, including which floors a compact lift would serve, and continue dialogue with the parties concerned.

If the barrier-free plan is not finalized, it will be difficult to begin construction on the wooden reconstruction. The project cost is already expected to exceed the original ceiling of 50.5 billion yen. The wooden reconstruction aims to revive, in wood and based on historical records, the keep destroyed in the war, and the question of barrier-free access has been a long-standing issue. How far the lift should reach overlaps with the question of how faithfully the historic keep should be reproduced.

Nagoya Castle was built by Tokugawa Ieyasu in 1612 (Keicho 17) and served in the Edo period as the residence of the Owari Tokugawa family. The keep was destroyed in an air raid in 1945 (Showa 20) and rebuilt in reinforced concrete in 1959 (Showa 34). Entry to the keep was suspended in May 2018 (Heisei 30), and work toward a wooden reconstruction is now under way. The golden shachi on the keep are known as the symbol of Nagoya Castle. Balancing a wooden reconstruction with barrier-free access is a representative topic when thinking about preserving and making use of cultural properties.

A draft barrier-free plan is scheduled to be presented at meetings from February next year onward. The timing of construction and the outlook for project costs will depend on future discussions. Before visiting Nagoya Castle, please check the latest information in Nagoya City's official guidance.
