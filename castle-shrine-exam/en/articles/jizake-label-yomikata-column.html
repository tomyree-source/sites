How to Read a Local Sake Label: A Beginner's Refresher on Seimai Buai and the Special Designation Names

The label on a bottle of local sake (jizake) is the “blueprint” of that sake. The variety of rice used, the seimai buai (rice polishing ratio), the special designation name, the sake meter value and acidity — once you know how to read them, the time spent hesitating in front of the shelf at the liquor store turns into study time. This time, based on the contents of the Jizake Exam textbook, we set out the basics of how to read a label. Sake is to be enjoyed only after you turn 20. The legal drinking age in Japan is 20. Please read this column as reading material for learning about jizake culture.

The seimai buai expresses the proportion of rice remaining after the brown rice has been polished. If it is 60%, it means that 40% has been polished away. The requirements are 50% or less for junmai daiginjo and daiginjo, 60% or less for the ginjo class, and 70% or less for honjozo. A smaller number is a guide to how much labor went into the brewing, but the merits of a sake are not decided by the number alone.

The special designation name on a label is decided by the seimai buai and by whether or not brewer's alcohol has been added. A sake with “junmai” attached must be made only from rice and rice koji, with no brewer's alcohol added. The tasting sets introduced on each regional page of the jizake guide, too, reveal differences in design from region to region when you view them while comparing the designation names on their labels.

The sake meter value (nihonshu-do) is an index based on specific gravity: the plus side is taken as a guide toward dry, and the minus side toward sweet. Acidity and amino acidity are also clues for knowing the tendencies of the flavor. However, these are only guides; the basic stance of label reading is to use numbers as “a ruler for comparing,” not as an “absolute evaluation.”

This covers the range of Chapters 3 and 5 of the Jizake Exam textbook. Be sure to have these four points down: “Junmai daiginjo has a seimai buai of 50% or less and no added brewer's alcohol”; “Honjozo is 70% or less”; “On the sake meter value, the plus side is a guide toward dry”; and “Hitohada-kan (skin-temperature warming) is about 35°C, and atsu-kan (hot warming) is about 50°C.”

Once you can read labels, the pages introducing the ten jizake regions will look different too. How do the characters of water and rice appear in the numbers and names? Please compare the correspondence between each producing region's character and its labels. The legal drinking age in Japan is 20.
