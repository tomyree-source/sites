Izumo Taisha and Kamiarizuki: The Month When the Gods Gather, at Japan's Great Matchmaking Shrine

Izumo Taisha, in the city of Izumo in Shimane Prefecture, enshrines Okuninushi Okami, known as a deity of nation-building and matchmaking. Even in the name of the tenth month of the old calendar, Izumo has a story different from other lands.

【Okuninushi Okami and Matchmaking】Okuninushi Okami, the enshrined deity of Izumo Taisha, appears in myth as a god of nation-building and has long been worshipped also as a god who ties the bonds between people. Weddings are held in the precincts, and the shrine bustles all year with worshippers seeking blessings for good matches. The main hall is built in the old taisha-zukuri style and is designated a National Treasure.

【Kannazuki and Kamiarizuki】Tradition says that in the tenth month of the lunar calendar, the gods from all over Japan gather in Izumo, so in lands left without their gods the month is called 'Kannazuki' — the month without gods. In Izumo, where the gods gather, the same month is called 'Kamiarizuki' — the month with the gods. This contrast is a representative exam point on the Shrine Certification, too.

【The Kamiari Festival and the Jukkusha】The gods are said to be welcomed at Inasa no Hama beach, and from around the 11th to around the 17th day of the tenth lunar month, the rites of the Kamiari Festival are held. During the festival, the gods are said to stay in the 'Jukkusha' (Nineteen Shrines) within the precincts, and worshippers join their hands in quiet prayer. The dates of the Kamiari Festival change from year to year, so check the official information before visiting.

【Four Claps in Prayer】Worship at Izumo Taisha is introduced as 'two bows, four claps, one bow.' The number of claps differs from the usual 'two bows, two claps, one bow.' The great shimenawa rope of the Kagura Hall is said to be about 13 meters long and weigh 5 tons — in person it is far larger than photographs suggest.

【Exam Study Points】This is covered in Chapter 5 of the Shrine Certification textbook. The three basics are: 'the deity of Izumo Taisha is Okuninushi Okami,' 'in Izumo the tenth lunar month is Kamiarizuki,' and 'the main hall is taisha-zukuri.' Remember also that the prayer uses four claps.

A trip that extends from Izumo Taisha to nearby Matsue Castle (a National Treasure with a surviving keep) is also recommended. You can enjoy this site's two themes — shrines and castles — in a single trip.
