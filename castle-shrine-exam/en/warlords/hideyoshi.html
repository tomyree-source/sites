Toyotomi Hideyoshi

豊臣秀吉 (とよとみ ひでよし)

Years1537–1598
HouseToyotomi clan
RegionKinki (Kansai)
EraAzuchi-Momoyama period

Overview

Born into a peasant family in Owari, he achieved a rise in the world said to have begun as Oda Nobunaga's sandal-bearer. After Nobunaga's death he defeated Akechi Mitsuhide, seized the reins of power through the Kiyosu Conference and the Battle of Shizugatake, became Imperial Regent (kanpaku), and unified the whole country.

Life

Born into a peasant family in Nakamura, Owari Province, he entered the service of Oda Nobunaga. In 1582, while commanding the Chugoku campaign against the Mori clan, he learned of the Honno-ji Incident; he quickly made peace with the Mori and rushed back to the Kyoto area in the celebrated 'Great Return from Chugoku' (Chugoku Ogaeshi), defeating Akechi Mitsuhide at the Battle of Yamazaki. After taking the lead of the Oda clan at the Kiyosu Conference and defeating Shibata Katsuie at the Battle of Shizugatake (1583), he built Osaka Castle while subjugating Shikoku and Kyushu in parallel. Appointed Imperial Regent, he achieved the unification of the country with the Odawara Campaign and the pacification of Mutsu in 1590. He advanced the separation of warriors and farmers through the Taiko land surveys and the sword hunt, but the invasions of Korea in his later years (the Bunroku and Keicho campaigns) became a great burden, and he died at Fushimi Castle in 1598.

In battle

Hideyoshi's generalship was characterized by an emphasis on logistics and stratagem. He piled up devices for winning without fighting: securing provisions in order to move large armies, starving castles out through siege, as in the flooding attack on Bitchu Takamatsu Castle, and breaking down enemy commanders through negotiation. The Odawara Campaign shows a political way of waging war, displaying an overwhelmingly large army so as to bring daimyo across the country into submission.

Arms and equipment

Few records survive of Hideyoshi's own martial skill, but from his youth he served close beside Nobunaga and is spoken of as a man whose talent for castle building and construction work was valued, as in the legend of the castle built overnight at Sunomata. The story that he built Sunomata Castle in a single night is considered an embellished version of an event in which a fort was in fact erected in a short period. He is also known for his skill at staging authority, in such things as the golden tea room and his horse standard of a thousand gourds.

Character and records

His mastery of people's hearts, described as that of a 'people charmer' (hitotarashi), is colored by many anecdotes, but most of those anecdotes were spread by the Edo-period Taikoki tales. His later years also left events on which assessments differ, such as the Hidetsugu Incident and the invasions of Korea. It is important to keep distinct the real figure based on the sources and the image of the Taiko shaped by storytelling and novels.

In literature

taikoki, kunidori, tokugawa

Shinsho Taikoki is an epic novel depicting Hideyoshi's life itself. In Kunidori Monogatari and Tokugawa Ieyasu as well, Hideyoshi is portrayed on a grand scale as Nobunaga's successor.
