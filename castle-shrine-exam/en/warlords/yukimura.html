Sanada Yukimura

真田幸村 (さなだ ゆきむら)

Years1567–1615
HouseSanada clan
RegionChubu
EraAzuchi-Momoyama period

Overview

The second son of Sanada Masayuki. His real name was Nobushige; the name 'Yukimura' was spread by Edo-period war chronicles. At the Winter Siege of Osaka he built the Sanada-maru and repelled the Tokugawa army, and at the Summer Siege he was killed in a charge that pressed close to Ieyasu's headquarters.

Life

Born as the second son of Sanada Masayuki, his name is given as Nobushige. After passing through the experience of being a hostage to the Uesugi house together with his father, he was raised as a retainer of a daimyo house in the age of Toyotomi Hideyoshi. At Sekigahara in 1600 he sided with the Western Army together with his father and held up Tokugawa Hidetada's great army at Ueda Castle (the Second Battle of Ueda). After the war he parted from his elder brother Nobuyuki and was exiled to Kudoyama in Kii Province, where he spent fourteen years. At the Winter Siege of Osaka in 1614 he entered Osaka Castle, built the outwork called the 'Sanada-maru,' and repelled the Tokugawa army's attacks. At the Summer Siege the following year, in the Battle of Tennoji-Okayama he made a charge that pressed close to Ieyasu's headquarters, and was killed in battle.

In battle

Yukimura's generalship was characterized by devices for facing a great army with a small force. At the Sanada-maru, he made use of the terrain and the structure of the outwork to withstand the Tokugawa side's attacks and inflicted heavy losses with volleys from his arquebus units. The charge at the Summer Siege was a bold one that aimed directly at the enemy headquarters in a battle with no line of retreat, and he is said to have forced Ieyasu to withdraw temporarily. However, many of the details of the fighting depend on accounts in war chronicles, and the range that can be confirmed in the sources is limited.

Arms and equipment

The six-coin crest (rokumonsen) known as Yukimura's banner emblem was the crest of the Sanada house, and it is spoken of as a symbol of his resolve, likened to the fare for crossing the Sanzu River (the river of the dead). He is known for troops equipped in red (aka-e), but as with the Ii house's red equipment, this has the aspect of having been impressed upon later times as the unified equipment of a unit. His image as a master of the jumonji (cross-shaped) spear is likewise a figure shaped by storytelling and novels.

Character and records

The assessment of him as 'the finest soldier in Japan' (Hinomoto-ichi no tsuwamono) is said to have spread after the Siege of Osaka. In Edo-period war chronicles such as the Sanada Sandaiki, Yukimura's story grew greatly, and fictional retainers such as the Sanada Ten Braves were also born. To savor this figure separately—as the historical Nobushige and as the Yukimura of story—is the key to reading him.

In literature

sanada, tokugawa, sekigahara

Sanada Taiheiki is the story of the Sanada house itself. Tokugawa Ieyasu depicts scenes of the Siege of Osaka, and Sekigahara depicts the age of the Battle of Ueda, in which his father Masayuki and the Sanada house were involved.
