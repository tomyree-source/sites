Akechi Mitsuhide

明智光秀 (あけち みつひで)

Yearsc. 1528–1582
HouseAkechi clan
RegionKinki (Kansai)
EraAzuchi-Momoyama period

Overview

Said to have been a native of Mino, he served as a senior retainer of Oda Nobunaga and was active in the Kinai region. He became lord of Sakamoto Castle and subjugated Tanba, but in 1582 he attacked Nobunaga at Honno-ji, and after being defeated by Toyotomi Hideyoshi at the Battle of Yamazaki, he died. His motive for the rebellion remains under debate today.

Life

Reliable sources on Mitsuhide's early life are scarce; tradition says he was of the Toki clan of Mino and served Saito Dosan, or that he wandered the provinces. After serving Ashikaga Yoshiaki he became a retainer of Oda Nobunaga and served in campaigns across the provinces, including the burning of Mount Hiei. Granted Sakamoto Castle in Omi and entrusted with the conquest of Tanba, he subjugated the province. In June 1582, while setting out as reinforcements for the Chugoku campaign, he changed course and attacked Nobunaga, who was lodged at Honno-ji Temple in Kyoto, driving him to suicide (the Honno-ji Incident). However, he was defeated at the Battle of Yamazaki by Hashiba Hideyoshi, who had hurried back from Bitchu Takamatsu, and died in the course of his flight.

In battle

Mitsuhide was a commander with a record of generalship sufficient to be entrusted with a regional army. In the conquest of Tanba he advanced the fighting against local lords (kokujin) such as the Hatano clan over several years, combining negotiation and siege warfare. The Honno-ji Incident was a lightning action that caught his lord unawares, but he failed in gathering allies afterward and collapsed before he could consolidate his regime. As for his motive, there are various theories—resentment, ambition, a mastermind behind the scenes—and no definitive answer has been reached.

Arms and equipment

Mitsuhide is also said to have been a master of the arquebus, and there is a story that he learned gunnery in his youth. He was also a man of culture, versed in waka poetry and the tea ceremony, and records of linked-verse gatherings he hosted remain. The opening verse 'Toki wa ima, ame ga shita shiru satsuki kana' ('Now is the time; the rain spreads beneath the sky, in the fifth month') is famous as having expressed his resolve for the Honno-ji Incident, but it is a verse upon which later interpretations have been layered.

Character and records

As the words 'a reign of three days' (Mikka Tenka) suggest, Mitsuhide's regime was short-lived. Both assessments exist—of him as a rebel, and of him as a resister against Nobunaga's severe rule—and his image has swung greatly with the times. In recent years research has also advanced on Mitsuhide as a man of practical affairs, including his domain management in Tanba and his command of his retainer band.

In literature

kunidori, taikoki, tokugawa

In Kunidori Monogatari he is depicted as a senior retainer of Nobunaga, and in Shinsho Taikoki as a party to the Honno-ji Incident and the Battle of Yamazaki.
