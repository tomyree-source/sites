Saito Dosan

斎藤道三 (さいとう どうさん)

Yearsc. 1494–1556
HouseSaito clan
RegionChubu
EraSengoku period

Overview

A representative figure of gekokujo (the low overthrowing the high), who rose from an oil seller to become the ruler of Mino. Tradition tells that he became a monk, then an oil merchant, then a warrior who stole a province. He formed an alliance with his son-in-law Oda Nobunaga, but fought his heir Yoshitatsu and was defeated and killed at the Battle of Nagara River.

Life

Dosan's early life is strongly colored by tradition and is told in the course of his becoming a monk, then an oil seller, then a retainer of the Saito clan, the deputy shugo of Mino, where he rose to prominence. In time he surpassed his lord's house, grasped the real power in Mino, and made Inabayama Castle (later Gifu Castle) his seat. He married his daughter Kicho (Nohime) to Oda Nobunaga and formed an alliance, and he is regarded as the man who saw through Nobunaga's talent. In his later years his relations with his heir, Saito Yoshitatsu, deteriorated, and at the Battle of Nagara River in 1556 he was defeated and killed in battle. As for Dosan's origins, there is also research holding that the seizure of the province took not one generation but two, and debate continues.

In battle

Dosan's wars rested on political strategy and marriage alliances. His way of advancing—intervening in the internal strife of the Toki house and grasping real power—is spoken of as a classic example of gekokujo, taking a province without fighting. At the Battle of Nagara River he was defeated before Yoshitatsu's numerically superior army, but the manner of his end is depicted in detail in war chronicles.

Arms and equipment

Dosan is known by the nickname 'the Viper of Mino' (Mino no Mamushi). The anecdote of the oil seller, in which he sold oil poured through the hole of a one-mon coin, and the episode in which Nobunaga, at their meeting, saw through his quality, are famous scenes found also in works such as the Shinchoko-ki, but their details include embellishment as well.

Character and records

Dosan has been depicted again and again in novels and dramas as a figure symbolizing the gekokujo of the Sengoku age. A story is also told that he sent Nobunaga a testament to the effect that he would yield the province to him. He is one of the commanders whose real image is difficult to fix, as the debate between the one-generation and two-generation theories continues in source research.

In literature

kunidori, futariJo, nobunagaGame

Kunidori Monogatari is an epic novel depicting Dosan's seizure of the province with him as the protagonist of its first half, and Futari Dosan is an epic novel depicting Dosan in his youth. Dosan is also included as one of the playable officers in the game Nobunaga's Ambition: Shinsei.
