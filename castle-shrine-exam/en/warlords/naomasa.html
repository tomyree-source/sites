Ii Naomasa

井伊直政 (いい なおまさ)

Years1561–1602
HouseIi clan
RegionKanto
EraAzuchi-Momoyama period

Overview

From the Ii house of Totomi, he served Tokugawa Ieyasu and became one of the Four Guardian Kings of the Tokugawa. He led the 'Ii Red Devils' (Ii no Aka-e), a unit organized from former Takeda retainers; at the Battle of Sekigahara he fought bravely as a vanguard commander and died from the wound he suffered there.

Life

Born as the son of Ii Naochika, a local lord (kokujin) of Totomi, after his father's death he passed a childhood moving between the Imagawa house and the Tokugawa house. Noticed by Tokugawa Ieyasu and entering his service, he rose to prominence and served at Nagashino and in the attack on Takatenjin Castle. He is also said to have accompanied Ieyasu in the crossing of Iga at the time of the Honno-ji Incident. At the Battle of Komaki-Nagakute he distinguished himself leading a red-equipped unit centered on former Takeda retainers. After the transfer to the Kanto region he became lord of Minowa Castle, and at the Battle of Sekigahara, amid the contest over the vanguard with Fukushima Masanori, he fought bravely and suffered a grave wound from gunfire. After the war he was granted the territory of Hikone, but died in 1602 as a result of the wound.

In battle

Naomasa's generalship was characterized by vanguard fighting that made use of the striking power of the Red Devils. At Sekigahara, his judgment concerning the dash for the van and his leading of the attack became one of the sparks of the main battle. It is also known that after the war he bore responsibility for postwar settlements and negotiations with daimyo, supporting Ieyasu in both military and diplomatic affairs.

Arms and equipment

The Red Devils were a unit whose equipment was unified in red, organized in a form inheriting the red equipment of Yamagata Masakage of the Takeda house. Few records survive of Naomasa's own martial skill, but he is a commander highly rated as a unit leader. As for the circumstances of the wound he received at Sekigahara, the widely known theory holds that he was shot amid the Shimazu army's fighting retreat (the 'Shimazu withdrawal').

Character and records

Naomasa enjoyed Ieyasu's deep trust and is spoken of by nicknames such as 'the Man-Slayer' and 'the Red Demon,' while he also had a shrewd side, being entrusted with negotiations with other daimyo. As the founder of the Ii house of the Hikone Domain, his descendants continue down to Ii Naosuke, the Great Elder (Tairo) of the late Edo period. He is one of the Four Guardian Kings whose early death is regretted.

In literature

sekigahara, tokugawa, nobunagaGame

Sekigahara depicts Naomasa's figure fighting bravely as a vanguard commander, and Tokugawa Ieyasu depicts his life as one of the Four Guardian Kings. He is not the protagonist of either work, but he appears as a major figure of the retainer band. Naomasa is also included as one of the playable officers in the game Nobunaga's Ambition: Shinsei.
