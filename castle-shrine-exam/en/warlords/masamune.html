Date Masamune

伊達政宗 (だて まさむね)

Years1567–1636
HouseDate clan
RegionTohoku
EraAzuchi-Momoyama period

Overview

The heir of Date Terumune of Mutsu. Tradition says he lost the sight in his right eye through a childhood illness, but he succeeded to the headship, defeated the Ashina clan at the Battle of Suriagehara, and spread his power across southern Mutsu. He joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Odawara Campaign in submission, and later became the first lord of the Sendai Domain.

Life

Born as the heir of Date Terumune, lord of Yonezawa Castle, he succeeded to the headship of the house at the age of eighteen. Taking his father's death as the occasion, he fought the Nihonmatsu clan, and at the Battle of Suriagehara in 1589 he defeated Ashina Yoshihiro and took Aizu into his hands. The following year, however, 1590, he was late in joining Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Odawara Campaign, and Aizu and other territories were confiscated. At the Battle of Sekigahara he attacked Uesugi territory on the Tokugawa side (the Keicho Dewa Campaign), and after the war he moved his seat from Iwadeyama to Sendai and laid the foundations of the Sendai Domain. In 1613 he sent the Keicho Embassy to Europe, dispatching Hasekura Tsunenaga and others to Rome. He remained the greatest daimyo of Mutsu under the Edo shogunate into his later years, and died in 1636.

In battle

Masamune's generalship was characterized by the speed with which he read the overall situation. At the Battle of Hitotoribashi he decided on a retreat from an inferior position, and at Suriagehara he seized the moment and defeated the Ashina army. At the same time, his judgments to submit to Hideyoshi and Ieyasu were also swift, and even while falling under suspicion over his handling of the Kasai-Osaki Rebellion he held fast to his territory; he was a commander gifted at the political judgments needed in order to survive.

Arms and equipment

When one speaks of Masamune, the symbol is his helmet, known for its large crescent-moon crest (the gengetsu-shaped maedate). As for the loss of sight in his right eye, tradition attributes it to smallpox in his childhood. The name 'Dokuganryu' (the One-Eyed Dragon) spread in later times. He is said to have been versed in horsemanship and archery as well, but some individual tales of his bravery contain much embellishment.

Character and records

Masamune was also a man of culture, versed in waka poetry and the tea ceremony, and in the building of Sendai's castle town he displayed his skill at city planning. His name is sometimes spoken of in connection with the origin of the word 'Date-otoko' (a stylish man). The contrast between his ambitious behavior when young and his caution after submitting to the Toyotomi and the Tokugawa is the key to reading his character.

In literature

date1, tokugawa, taikoki

Date Masamune is an epic series with Masamune as its protagonist. Tokugawa Ieyasu also depicts Masamune's movements in his later years. The protagonist of Shinsho Taikoki is Hideyoshi, but the scene in which Masamune joins the Odawara Campaign and decides the course of Mutsu is also depicted.
