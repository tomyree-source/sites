Oda Nobunaga

織田信長 (おだ のぶなが)

Years1534–1582
HouseOda clan
RegionChubu
EraAzuchi-Momoyama period

Overview

Rising from a minor daimyo family of Owari, he came to prominence after defeating Imagawa Yoshimoto at Okehazama. While reforming old customs through measures such as free markets (rakuichi-rakuza) and the abolition of toll barriers, he steadily expanded his power, built Azuchi Castle, and came within reach of unifying Japan, only to take his own life in the Honno-ji Incident.

Life

Born as the heir of Oda Nobuhide of Owari, he was sometimes called a 'fool' in his youth. After defeating Imagawa Yoshimoto at the Battle of Okehazama in 1560, he attacked the Saito clan of Mino, made Gifu Castle his seat, and began using the seal 'Tenka Fubu' (the realm spread under military rule). After escorting Ashikaga Yoshiaki to Kyoto, he later clashed with the shogun, and at the Battle of Nagashino (1575) he defeated the Takeda cavalry through the organized use of arquebus units. From 1576 he pressed ahead with the construction of Azuchi Castle, whose five-story keep is regarded as the starting point of early modern castle architecture. He expanded his territory into the Chugoku, Shikoku, and Hokuriku regions, and in 1582, on the verge of unifying the country, he was attacked at Honno-ji Temple by his retainer Akechi Mitsuhide and took his own life (aged 49).

In battle

His generalship was characterized by intelligence, mobility, and the adoption of new tactics. At Okehazama he won by a surprise attack exploiting heavy rain and the terrain; at Nagashino, by organized warfare combining anti-cavalry fences and volley fire from arquebusiers. At the same time, he was known for the severity of thorough suppression, as in the burning of Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei (1571) and the wars against the Nagashima Ikko-ikki. In the Ishiyama War he confronted the Hongan-ji for ten years, also showing a side that waged war through diplomacy and blockade.

Arms and equipment

Nobunaga is known as a commander who recognized the value of firearms early on and developed arquebus units. Among swords, the 'Soza Samonji,' said to have been obtained from Imagawa Yoshimoto, is known as a blade he prized. The image of Nobunaga as an innovator fond of imported (Nanban) armor and guns rests partly on missionary records and partly on later embellishment.

Character and records

Records such as those of the contemporary missionary Luis Frois portray him as rational and quick to make decisions. On the other hand, his harsh treatment of retainers and his mercilessness toward opposing forces are also recorded, and assessments of his character differ depending on the source. The story that he loved the song 'Atsumori,' known for the line 'a human life is but fifty years,' is a tradition spread through Edo-period war chronicles and similar works.

In literature

kunidori, tokugawa, taikoki

Kunidori Monogatari depicts Nobunaga as the central figure of its second half. In Tokugawa Ieyasu and Shinsho Taikoki as well, Nobunaga is portrayed on a grand scale as a figure who moved the age.
